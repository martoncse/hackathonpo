# Commodity notes: leads to verify, not facts

Read this in step 1. Each line is a starting point for research. Confirm it with a source before using it, or drop it.

## Lessons from the coffee and cocoa run (Colombia, Nov 2026)
- **Check national systems first.** The FNC's SICA already held about 1.8 million coffee lots, so "geolocation" was not the gap. The gap was paper receipts linked to plots, plus a volume check.
- **Offline field capture already exists** (FAO Ground, with Whisp built in). Don't pitch it as new.
- **Compute the volume rule on the measured boundary area**, use a current national yield, and label it "review". The first version used a stale average, a threshold that contradicted the README, and a demo farm whose mapped area made the flag disappear.
- **The importer fills the net weight.** Export an estimate under a different field name.
- **Whisp supports due diligence; it doesn't replace it** (FAO's own wording). Global forest maps over-flag shade coffee and cocoa, so a "high" result goes to review, not automatic exclusion.
- **Colombia is standard risk** (Impl. Reg. 2025/1093), so the simplified routes don't apply.
- **Don't claim smallholders are "dropped"** without a documented case. Say "reduces the risk of exclusion".

## Per commodity: leads
- **Cocoa:** CGIAR (2026) said Colombia had no national cocoa farm registry; check other countries' registries (e.g. Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire national traceability systems). Units: wet versus dry beans.
- **Coffee:** cherry, parchment (pergamino seco) and green coffee are different units; find a sourced conversion factor per country. National registries exist in some countries (SICA in Colombia).
- **Oil palm:** the chain runs from smallholder fresh fruit bunches (FFB) to the mill, then crude palm oil, then the refinery. Mill-level traceability and certification schemes (RSPO, national schemes) are already common. Convert yields between FFB/ha and oil.
- **Rubber:** smallholders sell cup lump through dealers; the chain is long and opaque. Convert dry rubber content.
- **Soya:** an annual crop, so check which Whisp field covers annual crops. Large farms dominate in some countries, which changes the smallholder story.
- **Cattle:** the geolocation duty covers **all establishments** where the animals were kept, not one plot. Animal-movement records (national livestock registries, transit permits) are the key paper trail. A yield-per-hectare rule doesn't transfer; look for stocking-density plausibility instead.
- **Wood:** forest concessions, harvest permits and species names matter. Legality evidence overlaps with existing timber-legality regimes, so check how they interact.

For every commodity, check whether the 2025/2650 amendments and later Commission guidance changed the scope, dates or simplifications.
