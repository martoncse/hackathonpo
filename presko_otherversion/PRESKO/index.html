How the rule works

The level comes from DepEd's 4H rule, which uses PAGASA's heat-index categories. Claude only reads building notes into the features below. The points are fixed and visible. Buildings never change the level: they flag the hot rooms to check first and the cooler rooms that give a school grounds to ask for face-to-face back. Nothing estimates a room temperature.

Three school inputs on top of the forecast. (1) An 8:00 indoor reading per building: a building reading a hotter level is emptied, and if every building reads hotter the school steps up. We assume "whichever is worse", since the memo text is unconfirmed. (2) The usual timetable: used on regular days and for the learner-hours baseline, and it flags shifts running through the afternoon peak. (3) The head's decision: recorded with name, time and reason. A level less protective than the rule needs SDO approval before the parent message counts as official. The log downloads as CSV for the SDO's situation report.

On a Hinay day, sections go into non-hot rooms at 06:00–10:00 and, if needed, 14:00–18:00. Nobody is in class 10:00–14:00. On a Hinga day, a school whose cooler rooms hold at least half its sections on those hours gets a face-to-face request drafted for the SDO. A separate checker then re-reads every assignment against the level.

What's real, what isn't

  • Real: 69 schools mapped in OpenStreetMap inside Iloilo City (HOT export on HDX), with one private school removed. DepEd lists 66 public schools, 75,890 public learners, 2,242 classrooms, and 18 schools on shifting schedules (TeacherPH, SY 2024-25; IMT News).
  • Real: the 4H levels in DepEd Memorandum 064 s. 2026, as reported (PNA, SunStar). We have not read the memo PDF itself.
  • Real, calibrated: the replay heat. It is the NWS heat index computed from Open-Meteo archive temperature and humidity, maximum over 11:00–16:00, plus +3.7°C (±2°C) fitted on 4 days PAGASA reported in April 2024. On the day, the coordinator types PAGASA's 5 p.m. forecast.
  • Real: 1 April 2024, when all 66 public schools suspended face-to-face classes on a 41°C forecast (GMA Regional TV). 3 Nov 2026, when 65 schools and 73,127 learners did so on a 43°C forecast (Daily Guardian).
  • Illustrative: building notes, room counts, sections, timetables (except Iloilo City NHS's reported 1:10–8:20 p.m. shift) and learner numbers. The division fills in its own. Readings and decisions you enter stay in this browser tab only.
  • Not free: the Hinay day is 4 hours instead of 6, and home-based work assumes modules or a connection at home. Under half of households had home internet in 2024.
