# Heat-in-schools tools and programmes, competitors and partners, and the cost of ADM / lost in-person days (for the Presko pitch)

Research date: 21 Nov 2026. Time-boxed (~18 tool calls). Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** come from secondary explainers or search snippets and were not checked against a primary document.

**Main point for the pitch:** As of Nov 2026, DepEd policy (DepEd Memorandum No. 064, s. 2026, the "4H framework") makes school heads choose each school's level (Hayo/Hinay/Hinga/Hinto) from classroom heat-index readings taken at 8am, 12nn and 2pm. I found no tool that gives a school a forecast-based next-day plan. The policy creates the decision; no product supports it. That gap is where Presko fits.

## 2. DepEd DRRMS tools and systems; is there a per-school heat decision tool?

### Takeaway
DepEd's heat governance changed quickly in 2024–2026: DO 22 s.2024, then reiteration memos in 2025, then **DepEd Memorandum No. 064, s. 2026 (issued 26 Oct 2026)**. That memo introduced the **4H framework (Hayo / Hinay / Hinga / Hinto)** and ended blanket nationwide heat suspensions. Each school decides from its own classroom readings. A draft executive order with automatic suspension at a heat index ≥40°C was under consultation (Jul 2026). No source mentions a DepEd per-school heat decision tool or dashboard. Measurement is manual and same-day, not forecast-based.

### Cited Findings
- **DepEd Order No. 22, s. 2024**, "Revised Guidelines on Class and Work Suspension in Schools during Disasters and Emergencies", is the base rule. — [SDO Muntinlupa memo NUM 2025-122](https://deped-muntinlupa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/NUM-2025-122.pdf)
- A March 2025 joint memorandum (OM-OSEC-OUOPS-2025-01-01456) reiterated DO 22 s.2024 "amid increased heat index levels". It directs DRRMS to issue periodic advisories and situation reports on school operations. — [Joint Memorandum PDF (SDO Nueva Vizcaya)](https://sdonuevavizcaya.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025-03-14-JOINT-MEMORANDUM-OM-OSEC-OUOPS-2025-01-01456-REITERATION-OF-DO-22-s.-2024-ON-CLASS-SUSPENSION-AND-STRENGTHENED-MONITORING-AMID-INCREASED-HEAT-INDEX-LEVELS.pdf)
- Division implementation in 2025 used bands of 38–40°C ("Extreme Caution") and ≥41°C ("Danger"). — [SDO Muntinlupa memo](https://deped-muntinlupa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/NUM-2025-122.pdf)
- March 2025: DepEd said there is "no automatic suspension of classes due to extreme heat". LCEs may suspend classes, and school heads may suspend in-person classes when there is no LGU declaration. DepEd also advised adjusting schedules, improving ventilation and using ADM. — [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2025/3/5/no-automatic-class-suspension); [DepEd](https://deped.gov.ph/?p=135861)
- 2024: DepEd said school heads can suspend in-person classes because of heat. A DepEd spokesperson defended letting heads decide on switching to online or offline home classes. — [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1926344/fwd-deped-on-class-suspensions-due-to-high-heat/amp); [Eco-Business](https://www.eco-business.com/news/first-covid-now-heat-online-schooling-returns-to-the-philippines)
- **DepEd Memorandum No. 064, s. 2026**, "Anticipatory Action, Preparedness, and Protective Measures for the 2026–2027 Super El Niño", was issued 26 Oct 2026 and took effect immediately. It runs until PAGASA declares the El Niño over. The 4H levels as reported **[UNVERIFIED: secondary explainer; thresholds conflict across outlets]**: Hayo <27°C (face-to-face with precautions); Hinay 27–32°C (shorter in-person hours or hybrid/home); Hinga 33–41°C (face-to-face suspended, lighter home-based work); Hinto ≥42°C (all academic activity suspended, including online and home tasks). Readings are taken indoors at 8:00, 12:00 and 14:00 against the PAGASA heat-index chart. — [DepEdClub explainer](https://depedclub.com/deped-heat-guidelines-2026-4h-class-suspensions/); [TeacherPH "Hayo, Hinay, Hinga, Hinto"](https://www.teacherph.com/walang-pasok/hayo-hinay-hinga-hinto/); [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/1007485/deped-classes-suspended-once-heat-index-hits-42degc-but-coordination-with-lgu-needed/story/)
- 4H is reported as aligned with **DepEd Order No. 014, s. 2026 on learning continuity in emergencies [UNVERIFIED: search summary]**. In Mandaue, school heads choose the classification and inform teachers, learners and parents at least a day before classes. — [Philstar, 13 Nov 2026 "DepEd adopts 4H heat guidelines"](https://www.philstar.com/nation/2026/11/13/2569839/deped-adopts-4h-heat-guidelines); [Inquirer "DepEd readies El Niño protocols"](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/?p=2327408)
- On 19 Oct 2026 (pre-issuance), Sec. Angara said school heads and division superintendents get "practical flexibility to exercise sound judgment based on actual ground conditions, classroom ventilation, and water availability". Schools must assess water supply, classroom heat, electricity and fire hazards, monitor classroom temperature and humidity, and prepare contingency feeding menus. No numeric triggers, dashboard or monitoring tool were named. — [Daily Tribune, 19 Oct 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/10/19/deped-to-allow-shorter-modified-class-days-as-el-niño-looms-2)
- Quezon City revoked its own MC No. 3 s.2025 (heat suspensions) to comply with DM 064 s.2026. — [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/1008199/qc-aligns-heat-related-class-suspension-rules-with-deped-marcos-eo/story/)
- Applied example: DepEd Tacloban suspended face-to-face classes in all public schools on 3–4 Nov 2026 at a 40°C heat index, citing the memo. — [Daily Tribune, 3 Nov 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/11/03/tacloban-citys-40c-heat-index-prompts-class-suspensions)
- A **draft executive order** (consultations began 29 Jul 2026) proposes automatic suspension of face-to-face classes at a heat index ≥40°C, official rainfall warnings, or air-quality/vog advisories, with automatic resumption once advisories lift. School heads could recommend campus-specific suspensions (public schools need the SDS's concurrence). One proposal gives LGUs one hour to act on school requests. Flexible delivery and SLMs continue instruction. Final status is not known as of Nov 2026. — [Daily Tribune, 29 Jul 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/07/29/deped-proposes-automatic-class-suspensions-at-40c-heat-index-hazard-alerts)
- Local rules vary. Pangasinan PDRRMO, for example, set automatic suspension above a 52°C heat index. — [GMA Regional](https://gmanetwork.com/regionaltv/news/107220/pangasinan-pdrrmo-sets-guidelines-on-class-suspension-due-to-heat/story)

### Inferences
- **The strongest framing for the pitch:** DM 064 s.2026 makes the school head the decision-maker and uses same-day indoor readings. Presko turns that into a *next-day*, forecast-based 4H recommendation that can be announced the evening before, which matches the Mandaue "inform a day before" practice. Map Presko's output labels onto Hayo/Hinay/Hinga/Hinto.
- The natural owner is DepEd DRRMS, which already issues advisories and situation reports. Presko could feed its sitreps with school-level logged decisions.
- **Risk:** the 4H thresholds as reported are unusually low (Hayo <27°C would cover almost every Philippine day). They may be misreported. Verify against the memo PDF before putting numbers on a slide.

### Gaps
- No primary PDF of DM 064 s.2026 or DO 014 s.2026 was retrieved.
- I did not research RADaR (Rapid Assessment of Damages Report), DepEd hazard maps or DRRMS dashboards in detail within the time box. No source named any DepEd per-school heat dashboard or app.
- Status of the draft heat/hazard EO after July 2026 is unknown.

## 3. Other heat early-warning or decision tools for schools (comparables, competitors, partners)

### Takeaway
Comparable responses elsewhere are blunt: blanket closures (Bangladesh 2024), state-level timing changes or early vacations (India), and humanitarian anticipatory-action protocols aimed at adults and cities, not schools (BDRCS heat EAP). The closest "tool" is guidance, not software: **CDRI Heat Smart Schools (Oct/Nov 2025)**, which cites **Arup/UCL UHeat**. I found no per-school, forecast-based, next-day heat planning tool anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- **CDRI "Heat Smart Schools: Guidance for building resilience to extreme heat"** (Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure, from its Community of Practice on extreme heat and education infrastructure; published 2025). It has seven steps: inclusive governance, science and forecasts, cooling measures, heat literacy, underlying risks (water, nutrition), financing, and preparedness (drills, recovery). It promotes linking schools with municipal, health and disaster systems. — [PreventionWeb listing](https://www.preventionweb.net/publication/documents-and-publications/heat-smart-schools-guidance-building-resilience-extreme-heat); [Guidance PDF](https://dymez6ioe12by.cloudfront.net/media/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/12150339/Heat-Smart-Schools_Guidance-Document.pdf); [CDRI webinar](https://www.preventionweb.net/event/webinar-heat-smart-schools)
- The CDRI guidance cites **UHeat**, a digital solution by ARUP, UCL and a university partner, as a heat tool. I did not confirm its full description. **[UNVERIFIED: excerpt truncated]** — [CDRI guidance PDF](https://dymez6ioe12by.cloudfront.net/media/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/12150339/Heat-Smart-Schools_Guidance-Document.pdf)
- A CDRI webinar notes that Asia accounted for 45% of global heat-related deaths in 2000–2019. — [PreventionWeb event](https://www.preventionweb.net/event/webinar-heat-smart-schools)
- SEforALL "Chilling Prospects": in the April 2024 heatwave across Asia and North Africa, more than 40 million children had education disrupted by heat closures. — [SEforALL data story](https://www.seforall.org/data-stories/chilling-prospects-data-story-sustainable-cooling-in-schools)
- **Bangladesh 2024:** all schools closed and 33 million children were out of classrooms, with temperatures near 42°C (Save the Children, Apr 2024). Closures were extended, nationwide, for up to two weeks in April–May. UNICEF figures vary: 33M in the Jan 2025 analysis, while UNICEF Bangladesh's headline says 35M for all climate shocks in 2024. — [Save the Children](https://www.savethechildren.net/news/bangladesh-extreme-heat-closes-all-schools-and-forces-33-million-children-out-classrooms); [Save the Children – extended closures](https://www.savethechildren.net/index.php/news/heat-stricken-bangladesh-extends-school-closures); [UNICEF Bangladesh](https://www.unicef.org/bangladesh/en/press-releases/33-million-children-bangladesh-had-schooling-disrupted-climate-crises-2024unicef)
- **Bangladesh Red Crescent heatwave Early Action Protocol (EAP)**, first activated in Dhaka on 21 Apr 2024, the first-ever Red Cross/Red Crescent heatwave EAP activation. Trigger: forecast max temperature >38°C, with humidity pushing the heat index above 38°C for ≥2 consecutive days (ECMWF/GFS). Funded from the IFRC DREF anticipatory pillar (CHF 499,930) to reach 124,000 people. Actions included warnings, drinking water and cooling stations. It does not target schools. BDRCS is exploring a National heat EAP with the DGHS. — [Climate Centre](https://www.climatecentre.org/13733/red-crescent-activates-early-action-protocol-for-heatwaves-in-dhaka/); [Anticipation Hub](https://anticipation-hub.org/news/the-bangladesh-red-crescent-society-activates-its-early-action-protocol-for-heatwaves-in-dhaka); [Rockefeller Foundation case study](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Heatwaves-in-Dhaka-Case-Study.pdf); [BDRCS AA page](https://bdrcs.org/anticipatory-action-aa/)
- **India 2024:** states changed school timings or brought vacations forward. Examples: Haryana (18–31 May, after an IMD directive), Himachal Pradesh (from 21 May, after an IMD alert), Jharkhand (from 22 Apr), Bihar (early vacation 15 Apr–15 May), and Kerala (classes moved 30 min earlier). This continued in 2025–2026; Noida cut hours in Apr 2026. — [India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/education/news/schools-in-these-states-to-remain-closed-due-to-intense-heat-wave-conditions-list-here-2024-05-20-932405); [Business Today 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/heatwave-warning-states-tweak-school-timings-delhi-kids-get-buttermilk-and-lemonade-471789-2025-04-12); [Business Today 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/india/story/heatwave-forces-noida-schools-to-cut-hours-new-timings-effective-immediately-check-revised-timings-for-other-states-527574-2026-04-27)
- Oxford Smith School commentary on India: "rethinking heat readiness beyond emergency response" in schools. — [Smith School](https://www.smithschool.ox.ac.uk/news/schools-warming-india-rethinking-heat-readiness-beyond-emergency-response)

### Inferences
- Positioning: the others either close everything (Bangladesh), change timings across a whole state (India), or protect adults in cities (BDRCS EAP). Presko is per-school, next-day and graded.
- Partners: CDRI (its framework step 2, "science and forecasts", is what Presko puts into practice); IFRC/Philippine Red Cross anticipatory-action community (Presko's threshold-plus-forecast logic resembles an EAP trigger); Arup (UHeat) as a possible data or method partner rather than a competitor.

## 4. Learning-loss evidence: learning poverty, modular learning quality, module costs, connectivity, EDCOM 2

### Takeaway
The baseline is very weak: **91% learning poverty** (World Bank, 2019 data, still the latest figure cited in 2024–2025). EDCOM 2 counted **53 lost teaching days in SY 2023–24**, about 30% of the 180 mandated days, from typhoons, heat, holidays and non-teaching tasks. The classroom shortage is **165,000 (2025)** and could reach **219,000 by 2028**. ADM fallbacks are costly and uneven: module printing needs were about **₱35B** against about ₱15B funded, COA flagged billions in deficiencies, and only **48.8% of households had home internet in 2024** (27.7% in BARMM).

### Cited Findings
- Learning poverty is 91% (share of 10-year-olds unable to read and understand an age-appropriate text). The World Bank still cited it in a Nov 2024 loan document and its 2025 country profile, as a 2019 measure. Comparators: Indonesia 53%, Thailand 23%. — [World Bank Philippines learning poverty profile (2025)](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/f7ba8fbb2c367f7f0089339b3c2b6f78-0140012025/original/Philippines.pdf); [BusinessWorld on the US$150M WB loan](https://wealthinsights.metrobank.com.ph/bworldonline/phl-seeks-150-m-wb-loan-to-improve-education-system); [ADB Philippines data](https://www.adb.org/where-we-work/philippines/data)
- EDCOM 2: 53 teaching days lost in SY 2023–2024, "equivalent to at least one full quarter". Causes were suspensions from typhoons, extreme heat, local holidays and teachers' non-teaching tasks. Public schools average 191 actual school days, and some regions lose up to 42 days. — [PIDS (in the news): "PISA results mirror PH education's lost days"](https://pids.gov.ph/details/news/in-the-news/pisa-results-mirror-ph-education-s-lost-days-grave-crisis); [PIDS press release on EDCOM II report](https://www.pids.gov.ph/details/news/press-releases/research-backed-edcom-ii-report-pushes-reforms-amid-ph-proficiency-collapse)

- Hedged illustration (my own arithmetic, not from a source): ₱35B for one year of 1:1 modules for about 27M learners is roughly ₱1,300 per learner per year. Use only as an order of magnitude.

## 5. Evidence that shortened in-person days beat full closure / value of instructional time

### Takeaway
Rigorous evidence shows that instructional time has a causal, positive but moderate effect on achievement, and that weather-driven lost days lower test scores. I found no study comparing *shortened* heat days against full closure. The argument that a shortened day beats closure is an inference from instructional-time evidence plus the poor quality of the home-based alternative.

### Cited Findings
- Lavy (2015, Economic Journal; NBER w16227) uses within-student, across-subject variation in PISA 2006 (50+ countries) and finds that weekly instructional hours have a positive causal effect on achievement, with larger effects in developed countries. — [NBER w16227](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16227/revisions/w16227.rev1.pdf)
- A replication using five more PISA waves (2000–2018) finds a "qualitatively similar but smaller" effect. — [Lund University WP 2020:18](https://lup.lub.lu.se/search/files/194592145/WP20_18.pdf)
- Marcotte & Hemelt use snow-induced school closures in Maryland as a natural experiment and find that unscheduled closure days lower test scores. — cited in [Lavy, "Time on task" (Warwick)](https://text.www2.warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/economics/staff/vlavy/text_and_tables_time_on_task_09_august_2018.pdf); see also [IZA DP 9797](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/9797)
- Caveat: the effect of time depends on instruction quality and classroom environment, which is relevant in a hot classroom. — [Spain INEE review of instruction-time studies](https://educacionfpydeportes.gob.es/inee/va/dam/jcr:c8a1353b-1ae3-4633-bfa3-076a92c29bad/instructiontimesrjsfeb13.pdf)

### Inferences
- The pitch can say that instructional time causally raises learning, that unplanned closures cut scores, and that the home-based fallback reaches about half of households online and is weak on paper. So keeping a shortened, cooler-hours in-person day (4H "Hinay") when it is safe should lose less learning than full suspension. Present this as reasoned, not proven.
- Heat itself lowers learning (Park et al. on US PSAT heat exposure is well known), so the trade-off is two-sided. Presko's value is choosing correctly, not maximising days. *[Park et al. not retrieved in this session — UNVERIFIED citation; verify before use.]*

### Gaps
- No direct evidence comparing shortened heat days with closure was found.
- I did not retrieve exact effect sizes (for example, per extra hour or per lost day) from Lavy or Marcotte–Hemelt. Check the papers before quoting numbers.
- Global estimates of lifetime earnings losses from COVID-era closures (World Bank/UNICEF/UNESCO "State of the Global Education Crisis") were not retrieved this session.
