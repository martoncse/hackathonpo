# Rebuild Presko around DepEd's 4H levels

Presko's current engine and pitch were built for a rule that no longer applies. Under **DepEd Memorandum No. 064, s. 2026** (released 26 Oct 2026), the heat index now maps to four levels: **Hayo below 27°C, Hinay 27–32°C, Hinga 33–41°C and Hinto at 42°C or above**. At Hinga, face-to-face (F2F) classes are fully suspended. At Hinto, home-based work stops too. The prototype's headline demo, "44°C tomorrow, so keep a morning shift and save 85% of learner-hours", therefore recommends something the rule forbids. Its "Replay 30 April 2024" says Iloilo was "just under the threshold" on a day PAGASA forecast 44°C. The research points to one central change: make the 4H level the backbone of the engine, and reposition Presko as the day-before decision layer that 4H lacks. That layer has four parts: a forecast level for each school, building-based flags that tell the head which rooms to check first and which schools have grounds to ask the SDO to bring F2F classes back, a split 06:00–10:00 / 14:00–18:00 schedule for Hinay days, and a verified parent message that goes out before 5 p.m. Heat must come from a Rothfusz heat index computed from Open-Meteo temperature and humidity, plus a calibrated offset of about +3.7°C. Open-Meteo's own "apparent temperature" should no longer be used. The replay should move to **1 April 2024**, when **66 of 66 SDO Iloilo City public schools** suspended classes. The competitive claim has to change too: DepEd's **Project LIGTAS+** (launched Aug 2026) already targets school-level suspension decisions, so Presko should be pitched as a heat-specific module that works with it.

## DM 064's four levels replace the "about 40°C" rule

All outlets that report numbers give the same four bands, and those bands are exactly PAGASA's heat-index categories, so they are not misreported. **Hinay** allows a shortened F2F day, for example **6–10 a.m. and 2–6 p.m.**, or hybrid learning. **Hinga** (33–41°C, *or loss of the school's water supply*) suspends F2F entirely and moves learners to light modular or DepEd TV/radio work. **Hinto** (≥42°C) suspends "both face-to-face and home-based academic activities" ([DepEd Tambayan](https://www.depedtambayanph.net/2026/10/deped-memo-064-s-2026-el-nino-4h-scenario.html); [astig.ph](https://astig.ph/deped-memorandum-64-heat-class-suspension-42-degrees-2026/); [PNA](https://www.pna.gov.ph/article/122-w-visayas-public-schools-suspend-in-person-classes-due-to-extreme-heat)). The memo stays in force until PAGASA declares the 2026–27 El Niño over. It also asks schools to take calibrated indoor readings at **8:00, 12:00 and 14:00** ([DepEdClub](https://depedclub.com/deped-heat-guidelines-2026-4h-class-suspensions/)). SDO Iloilo City's own legal officer described it in these terms on local radio: 33–41°C means "otomatiko nga suspended ang face-to-face classes", classes continue "paagi sa alternative learning nga amo ang modular kag online", and schools "mahimo man nga magpangabay … nga ibalik ang face-to-face classes", meaning they may request that F2F be restored ([Bombo Radyo Iloilo](https://iloilo.bomboradyo.com/mga-eskwelahan-sa-siyudad-sang-iloilo-otomatiko-nga-ma-suspend-ang-klase-kun-maglab-ot-sa-42-degrees-celsius-ang-heat-index-legal-officer-sang-schools-division-of-iloilo-city/)).

The decision sits with the school head, but not alone. Heads "may assess actual conditions on their campuses" but "must inform their respective schools division superintendents" ([Daily Guardian](https://www.dailyguardian.com.ph/blog/extreme-heat-suspends-classes-in-122-western-visayas-schools)). Usec. Garma adds that "the school head cannot just decide to send students home": the SDO vets the decision ([astig.ph citing Rappler](https://astig.ph/deped-memorandum-64-heat-class-suspension-42-degrees-2026/)). In practice the trigger has been the **PAGASA next-day forecast**, not the indoor readings. On **3 Nov 2026**, 122 Western Visayas public schools suspended F2F classes on forecasts, and **SDO Iloilo City accounted for 65 schools and 73,127 learners** on a 43°C forecast ([Daily Guardian](https://www.dailyguardian.com.ph/blog/extreme-heat-suspends-classes-in-122-western-visayas-schools); [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/nation/2026/11/03/2567603/43c-extreme-heat-disrupts-classes-western-visayas)). In Mandaue, heads set the next day's level between 4 and 5 p.m. using hygrometers and forecasts, then notify parents ([SunStar Cebu](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/deped-mandaue-implements-4-level-heat-index-framework-to-protect-students)). That afternoon window is exactly Presko's moment. The primary PDF of DM 064 returned 403, so one question remains open: does the binding level come from the indoor reading, the forecast, or whichever is worse? Presko should therefore label its output a **forecast level to confirm with the 8:00 reading**.

Two higher layers sit on top of DM 064. **Executive Order No. 130, s. 2026** (signed 28 Oct, made public 3 Nov, amending EO 66 s. 2012) sets out three tiers of suspension: *automatic* on PAGASA warnings or advisories, *localized* by the local chief executive, and *granular* by the school after a site assessment ([Manila Times](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/11/04/news/national/palace-releases-class-suspension-guidelines/2460865); [GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/1008199/qc-aligns-heat-related-class-suspension-rules-with-deped-marcos-eo/story/)). No source that could be read confirms its numeric heat trigger. The July 2026 draft proposed ≥40°C ([Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/07/29/deped-proposes-automatic-class-suspensions-at-40c-heat-index-hazard-alerts)), and the implementing guidelines are probably still pending. The city overlay is **Iloilo City EO 24 s. 2025**. It keys on PAGASA's 5 p.m. advisory the day before, with 40°C optional and 42°C automatic ([GMA Regional TV](https://www.gmanetwork.com/regionaltv/news/106983/new-protocols-issued-on-class-suspension-in-iloilo-city-due-to-heat-index/story)). Its automatic threshold was later reported as 41°C ([GMA Regional TV](https://www.gmanetwork.com/regionaltv/news/107479/iloilo-city-cancels-inperson-classes-due-to-41c-heat-index/story)) and as "lowered from 42°C to 40°C" in a July 2026 UN ESCAP workshop report, which still lists it as active ([UN ESCAP](https://www.unescap.org/sites/default/d8files/event-documents/Meeting%20Report,%20Iloilo%20Urban%20Heat%20Action%20Planning%20Workshop,%2024-25%20June%202026.pdf)). The legal chain to cite is **EO 130 s. 2026 → DO 22 s. 2024 → DM 064 s. 2026**. DO 37 s. 2022, which the prototype cites twice, was reportedly repealed by DO 22 s. 2024 ([Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2024/12/27/dep-ed-updates-class-suspension-guidelines)). It is valid only as the 2024 historical basis.

The rule set to encode is: `level = hi < 27 ? HAYO : hi < 33 ? HINAY : hi < 42 ? HINGA : HINTO`. Values from 32.0 to 32.9 count as Hinay and 41.0 to 41.9 as Hinga. Loss of water forces at least Hinga. A configurable city override (default 42°C, options 40/41) forces F2F suspension citywide. A configurable EO 130 automatic trigger, shown as "implementing guidelines pending", does the same. Each output is a recommendation to the school head, confirmed with the SDO.

## April in Iloilo is never below Hinga, calibrated or not

PAGASA computes heat index with the **Rothfusz (1990) regression**, which has a stated error of ±1.3°. It reports the daily **computed maximum** for each synoptic station and builds its 2-day forecast from ECMWF ([PAGASA lecture](https://pubfiles.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/climps/climateforum/speciallecture.pdf)). Open-Meteo's `apparent_temperature`, which the prototype uses now, is a different quantity: a Steadman/BoM apparent temperature with wind and solar terms ([Open-Meteo source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open-meteo/open-meteo/main/Sources/App/Helper/Meteorology.swift)). In April 2024 it happened to land within about ±1.5°C of the heat index, but that agreement is coincidental. The engine should compute the NWS algorithm itself from hourly `temperature_2m` and `relative_humidity_2m` ([NWS WPC](https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/html/heatindex_equation.shtml)) and take the **daily maximum over 11:00–16:00**. Across all 30 days of April 2024, the peak fell in that window, most often at 13:00.

Raw gridded heat index runs well below PAGASA. The best-match/IFS series (the same model family PAGASA forecasts from, and what the Open-Meteo forecast API serves) gives **37.7–40.7°C on days PAGASA reported 42–44°C**. The likely cause is midday humidity in the grid that is too low for a coastal station. ERA5 comes closer on those days but runs about 2.5°C warmer on every day, sits on an inland grid cell, and is archive-only, so it should not be used. On the four observed matches (17, 21, 22 and 28 April) the IFS shortfall averages **+3.7°C**, ranging from 3.0 to 4.3 ([Inquirer, 17 Apr](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1930636/heat-index-april-17-42c-to-44c-temperature-in-17-areas); [Inquirer, 21 Apr](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1932039/heat-index-recorded-on-sunday-april-21); [MB, 22 Apr](https://mb.com.ph/2024/4/22/dangerous-heat-index-recorded-in-pasay-city-25-more-areas-on-april-22-1)). A useful check: with that offset applied, the series matches PAGASA *forecasts* that were not used to fit it. **1 April gives 41.2 against PAGASA's 41**, **25 April 42.7 against 43** and **30 April 44.2 against 44** ([IMT News](https://imtnews.ph/in-person-classes-in-iloilo-city-suspended-due-to-intense-heat); [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/weather/content/904663/pagasa-danger-level-heat-index-possible-in-38-areas-on-april-25/story/); [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/weather/content/905156/danger-level-heat-index-in-39-areas-on-tuesday-april-30-2024-pagasa/story/)). The offset still rests on four or five news-quoted values, and the PAGASA values were seen only in search summaries. Ship it as "+3.7°C, calibrated on 4 PAGASA days, ±2°C" and make the parameter editable.

The calibrated series changes the story the prototype tells. The raw IFS heat index for April 2024 ranges from **36.4 to 41.3°C**, so every day is Hinga even with no correction. The calibrated series puts **19 days at Hinga and 11 at Hinto, with none at Hayo or Hinay**. Read literally, DM 064 would have kept Iloilo City's public classrooms closed to F2F classes for the whole of April 2024. The "Normal April day · 34°C" preset is therefore misleading. 34°C is not a normal Iloilo April afternoon, and under 4H it already suspends F2F. Presko's value in this month cannot be "keep the morning shift". It has to be three things: an honest level, an exception case for cool buildings, and a fast and trustworthy parent message.

## Replay 1 April 2024: 66 of 66 schools, forecast 41°C

**1 April 2024 is the only date with a sourced, city-level school count.** DepEd data as of 3 p.m. that day showed **66 Iloilo City schools** among more than 800 in Western Visayas that had suspended in-person classes ([GMA Regional TV](https://www.gmanetwork.com/regionaltv/news/101185/over-800-schools-in-western-visayas-suspend-inperson-classes/story)). Mayor Treñas's **EO No. 047** suspended F2F classes from preschool to SHS for 1–2 April on a PAGASA forecast of **41°C**. The suspension would continue while the forecast stayed at or above 40°C ([IMT News](https://imtnews.ph/in-person-classes-in-iloilo-city-suspended-due-to-intense-heat)). The amendment **EO 047-A** let schools able to handle the heat, such as air-conditioned private schools, hold onsite classes ([SunStar](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/iloilo/treñas-extends-suspension-of-face-to-face-classes-in-iloilo-city-due-to-extreme-heat-conditions)). That amendment is a sourced precedent for exactly what Presko's building layer does: separate schools that can safely hold F2F classes from those that cannot. No learner count exists for any 2024 suspension day, so the replay must show "≈76,000 learners (estimate from SY 2024-25 enrolment)". The 30 April replay should be dropped as the headline. PAGASA forecast **44°C** for Iloilo that day, not something "below 40", and the closure came from a national order shared with a transport strike ([Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1934644/in-person-classes-in-public-schools-suspended-over-heat-transpo-strike)), so it says little about local decisions.

A stronger, more current second card is **3 Nov 2026**: 65 schools and **73,127 learners**, both sourced, on a 43°C forecast that means Hinto under the live 4H rule ([PNA](https://www.pna.gov.ph/article/122-w-visayas-public-schools-suspend-in-person-classes-due-to-extreme-heat)). The heat values for that date have not been pulled yet. Fetching them takes one Open-Meteo archive call, and the date proves the tool targets the rule in force today.

For the division baseline, **SDO Iloilo City has 66 public schools, 75,890 public learners, 2,980 teachers and 2,242 classrooms**, which works out to about 34 learners per room. **18 public schools ran shifting schedules** in SY 2024-25 ([TeacherPH, DepEd data as of Oct 2025](https://www.teacherph.com/schools/divisions/iloilo-city); [IMT News](https://imtnews.ph/18-public-schools-in-iloilo-city-implement-shifting-schedules-due-to-classroom-shortage)). At Iloilo City NHS, 6,664 learners were short 60 classrooms, and **Grades 9–10 attended 1:10–8:20 p.m.**, straight through the afternoon heat peak ([GMA Regional TV](https://www.gmanetwork.com/regionaltv/news/103170/classroom-shortage-observed-anew-on-first-day-of-classes/story)). The prototype maps **70 OpenStreetMap points**, labels them "70 public schools", and includes "CPU Elementary School", which is probably part of the private Central Philippine University (an inference). It should say "70 schools mapped in OSM; DepEd lists 66 public schools" and filter out obvious private entries.

## Parent messages need an official-source line, sent the evening before

The documented channels in Iloilo are **city executive orders posted on the City Government's Facebook page**, the Mayor's Viber statements to media, and Hiligaynon radio such as Bombo Radyo ([Manila Times](https://manilatimes.net/2024/04/26/regions/classes-halted-anew-in-iloilo-city-schools/1943313); [Bombo Radyo Iloilo](https://iloilo.bomboradyo.com/mga-eskwelahan-sa-siyudad-sang-iloilo-otomatiko-nga-ma-suspend-ang-klase-kun-maglab-ot-sa-42-degrees-celsius-ang-heat-index-legal-officer-sang-schools-division-of-iloilo-city/)). Announcements follow PAGASA's 5 p.m. advisory. DepEd keeps disowning fake "Walang Pasok" pages and an AI-generated fake suspension video, and tells parents to trust only official channels ([Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2026/03/12/deped-warns-public-vs-fake-post-claiming-thursdayfriday-class-suspension); [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/953986/deped-warns-public-vs-fake-ai-generated-class-suspension-video/story)). A message that names its source (the school head, SDO Iloilo City and the PAGASA forecast value) is a feature in its own right. SMS blasts and class group chats are not documented for Iloilo schools, so present them as design assumptions.

The one verified Hiligaynon register code-switches heavily. It uses "ma-suspend ang klase", "suspended ang face-to-face classes", "padayon sa gihapon ang klase" and "magapadayon ang klase paagi sa … modular kag online", and keeps *heat index*, *face-to-face*, *modular* and *online* in English. Official instruments are in English. Presko should ship fixed Hiligaynon templates in that register rather than a free AI translation. For Hinga: "PAHIBALO: Base sa PAGASA 5 PM heat index forecast (43°C), suspended ang face-to-face classes sa [School] buwas, [petsa]. Magapadayon ang klase paagi sa modular/online. — [School Head], SDO Iloilo City". Each template should have an English pair and be checked by a native speaker. "Wala klase" and the "hasta 12:00 NN lang" phrasing remain unverified.

## LIGTAS+ is the platform; Presko is the heat module

The claim "no such tool exists" is no longer safe. **DepEd Project LIGTAS+**, built by DepEd DRRMS with ECAIR and launched on 26 Aug 2026, aims for "more granular … data-based decision-making" so that suspensions and resumptions can be decided school by school. It cites heat index as one example input, and its recommendations are "still being reviewed" ([GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/scitech/technology/999947/deped-s-project-ligtas-aims-for-school-level-class-suspensions-resumptions/story/)). No coverage shows next-day heat forecasting, a mapping to 4H, building-level flags or parent messaging. The defensible line is: "LIGTAS+ is building school-level hazard data. Presko is the heat-specific, next-day, 4H plan plus the parent message, and it can plug into it." The same office, DRRMS, owns both, which makes the deployment story stronger. The other pitch figures check out with corrections. **91% learning poverty is 2019 data** published in 2022, not a 2022 measurement. "Up from 69.5%" mixes two methods and must not be shown as a trend, and "67%" is a regional threshold, not a Philippine rate ([World Bank brief](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/f7ba8fbb2c367f7f0089339b3c2b6f78-0140012025/original/Philippines.pdf)). UNICEF's **242 million / 171 million from heatwaves / 74%** figures are confirmed, as is **118 million in April 2024 alone**. UNICEF gives no Philippines-only count ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/nearly-quarter-billion-childrens-schooling-was-disrupted-climate-crises-2024-unicef)). **7,188 of 47,678 schools on 25 April 2024** holds ([Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1933768/deped-on-adm-schools-on-thursday)).

What not to claim follows from the same evidence. Do not say Presko keeps classrooms open on Hinga or Hinto days, that it would have kept 85% of learner-hours on 30 April 2024, or that Iloilo was below any threshold that day. ## Conclusion

The rule change turns Presko's weakness into its case. Under the 4H rule, the expensive moment is no longer choosing between "full day" and "closed". It is making 66 correct, SDO-vetted, day-before level calls every hot afternoon, from forecasts known to be gridded and biased, and getting a trusted message to parents before fake pages do. Calibrated data shows an Iloilo April would sit at Hinga or Hinto every day. The levers that matter are therefore the exception (which schools can justify asking for F2F back, a pattern the city itself used in EO 047-A) and the shoulder months when Hinay's split schedule applies. A per-room "morning shift at 44°C" is not one of them.

Two uncertainties should shape what the team says next. The DM 064 text was never read directly, so whether indoor readings or the forecast bind is unresolved. And the +3.7°C offset rests on four news-quoted points. Both are cheap to fix in a pilot: one SDO conversation and a TAMSS data request for Iloilo station records. That gives the pitch an honest "week 1" that a public-sector jury can believe.
