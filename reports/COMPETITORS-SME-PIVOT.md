# Competitors and the EU-SME pivot: who can take this market, how to outflank them, and what to change

*Competitor agent, 21 Nov 2026 (Sciences Po AI Hackathon, pitch day). This builds on `MOAT-ANALYSIS.md` (the competitor map and §9 passport strategy) and does not repeat it. Every claim has a link.*

*Tags used below:*
- ***Unverified***: the page wasn't readable, the source is secondary, or the claim is the vendor's own marketing.
- ***Not found***: searched for and nothing came up.
- ***Our arithmetic***: a figure we derived by combining sources.

---

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Recommendation: option (c), two-sided, with the EU SME importer as the paying customer.** The cooperative issues passports for free. The micro or small EU importer pays per verified shipment. The importer is the operator who files the DDS and carries the liability. The pitch should therefore be told **from the importer's desk**: "a passport arrives, you check it, you file it." It should not be told from the cooperative's yard. That keeps 100% of the existing build and changes the story, the customer and the price line. It adds about 4–6 hours of buyer-side features (§6).
2. **Narrow the segment hard.** Most "EU SMEs" are **not** customers:
   - SME downstream operators and traders (a roaster or chocolatier buying from an EU importer) now have almost no obligations. They don't file a DDS and don't register ([COM(2026) 191, p.10](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-8943-2026-INIT/en/pdf); [Coolset SME guide](https://www.coolset.com/academy/eudr-reporting-guide-for-smes)).
   - SME importers from **low-risk** origins get Art 13 simplified due diligence, with no risk assessment or mitigation unless something points to risk ([COM(2026) 191, §4.1.3](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-8943-2026-INIT/en/pdf)).
   - **The wedge is the micro or small first placer of cocoa from a standard-risk origin (Côte d'Ivoire), buying from independent cooperatives.** Direct-trade bean-to-bar makers who import themselves are the sharpest sub-niche.
3. **Biggest new threat: Epoch** (London, $4.25M seed, self-serve SME EUDR product launched 26 Oct 2026). Close behind are **LiveEO TradeAware Professional** (€3,990/yr, self-serve since Sep 2026) and **Fairtrade Fairtrace data sharing** (live 2 Nov 2026, free, but only for certified chains).
4. **Biggest risk: we have zero evidence that SME importers will pay per shipment, and a two-sided product starts cold.** An importer gets nothing until its cooperative issues passports. Say this on stage as the thing the next 6 weeks will test.

## 1. The SME segment in detail

**What this means for us:**
- Côte d'Ivoire is **standard risk** ([Impl. Reg. 2025/1093](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R1093)), so a small EU importer of Ivorian cocoa still owes the **full** Art 9–11 file.
- That file is exactly what our passport and pack feed.
- The "51% of importers qualify for Art 13" figure (unverified in `MOAT-ANALYSIS.md` §8) can now be traced. It is the **Commission's own modelling assumption**: "51% instead of 20% previously, of the importing operator base is assigned half of the standard compliance cost" ([COM(2026) 191 p.22](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-8943-2026-INIT/en/pdf)). It's a model input, not a count of firms. Quote it that way.

### 1d. Pain points (evidence)

The best SME-specific data is the **LIFE FairTHRIVE / Max Havelaar France survey (Oct 2025)** of **128 companies, mostly cocoa and coffee SMEs** ([Max Havelaar France](https://www.fairtrade.net/maxhavelaarfrance-fr/engagement-entreprises/service-ressources/soutien-rdue-pour-les-entreprises/rdue-dans-quelle-mesure-les-pme-ue-sont-elles-preparees.html)):
- Average readiness was **32%**, and **no company was fully ready**.
- **68%** call themselves beginners.
- Only **12.6% collect geolocation data**.
- More than a third have **no formal data system** for due diligence.
- More than half have **no risk-assessment procedure or mitigation plan**.
- Many don't know whether they're in scope.

Other evidence:
- **Enforcement focuses on the evidence.** Dutch NVWA dry runs found most operators lacked enough information behind their DDS ([secondary summary](https://www.interu.io/blog/eudr-enforcement-is-here-what-timber-importers-need-to-know-and-do), already in MOAT §9). From 30 Dec 2026 the NVWA checks "whether a shipment's information is reliable and traceable" to the geolocation ([ondernemersplein.overheid.nl](https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/duurzaam-ondernemen/milieu/verordening-ontbossingsvrije-producten-eudr-wat-betekent-dit-voor-u/)).
- **Supplier data quality** is a common problem: reversed coordinates, points in the sea, overlapping polygons ([Crowe](https://www.crowe.com/pl/en-us/insights/eudr---10-implementation-challenges)).
- **Supply of compliant cocoa:** "EU importers won't be able to get enough compliant cocoa from indirect or third-party shippers" ([TimesLive/Reuters](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/2026-08-17-chocolate-could-get-pricier-as-eu-scrutinises-west-african-cocoa/), in MOAT).

### Recommendation: **(c), told as (b)**

- **The customer is the micro or small EU first placer of standard-risk cocoa.** The cooperative is the free issuing side.
