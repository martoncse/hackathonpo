## Q1. Per-category table: Colombian legal sources, evidence, checklist questions and red flags

### Takeaway
Colombian law can be mapped onto every Art 2(40) category. For a smallholder coffee or cocoa plot, though, the evidence that actually matters reduces to a short list:
- a tenure document (one of about 8 accepted types);
- a geospatial check against the UPRA Frontera Agrícola, Ley 2 de 1959 reserve zones, RUNAP protected areas and ethnic territories;
- a water-use registration or VRD status;
- a family-labour declaration, plus no-child-labour controls;
- a RUT.

Commission guidance says that if national law does not require a title to produce and sell, the EUDR does not require one either. Where the first review shows negligible risk, plot-by-plot legal documents are not required.

### Cited Findings

**How the EU frames the test (applies to all categories)**
- A law is "relevant" only if it specifically affects the legal status of the production area. Its content must be directly linked to halting deforestation and forest degradation, or to the climate and biodiversity goals in Art 1(1). Laws that apply to supply chains in general do not qualify on that basis alone. — [Guidance C/2026/3896 §6(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2026/3896/oj/eng)
- The guidance gives illustrative scopes for agricultural commodities:
  - land use rights: land transfer and lease rules;
  - environment: protected areas, wildlife, land development;
  - forest rules: forest protection and harvesting rights within gazetted boundaries;
  - third parties: tenure, usufruct and traditional land use of indigenous peoples and local communities;
  - labour and human rights: "where relevant to the EUDR's objectives";
  - FPIC: projects affecting indigenous land;
  - tax, anti-corruption, trade and customs: only with a specific link to EUDR objectives, or where the rule concerns agricultural or timber production specifically.

  — [Guidance §6(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2026/3896/oj/eng)
- On evidence, the guidance says:
  - Information must be "adequately conclusive and verifiable" (Art 9(1)(h)).
  - In-depth collection should be focused on higher-risk supply chains. If the risk is negligible, operators need not collect per-plot legal documents or specific titles.
  - A title is required only if domestic law requires one to produce and sell.
  - Acceptable sources: permits, registries, contracts (including with indigenous and local communities), certification, court decisions and audits. Producer self-declarations and social agreements may also help.
  - Operators should check that documents are consistent with each other. High corruption levels may call for further verification.

  — [Guidance §6(b)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2026/3896/oj/eng)
- EFI says Art 2(40) has **seven** areas for agricultural products: category (c), forest rules, concerns wood harvesting. The EFI tool groups them into 6 chapters: 1 Land use; 2 Environment (forest included); 3 Third-party rights and FPIC; 4 Labour; 5 Human rights; 6 Tax, anti-corruption, trade and customs. — [EFI palm tool](https://fedepalma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ANO-2026-FINAL-Guia-legalidad-palma-aceite-EUDR_Colombia.pdf)
- EFI rates most requirements as having a "high implementation level" and recommends "diligencia debida aligerada" (light due diligence). It notes that "the absence of documentary evidence does not imply illegality", because a document is not required for tenure to be legal in Colombia. — [EFI palm tool, Cat. 1](https://fedepalma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ANO-2026-FINAL-Guia-legalidad-palma-aceite-EUDR_Colombia.pdf)
- Colcocoa lists 8 legality components and gives a smallholder evidence option for each (details in the table below). — [Colcocoa ruta](https://www.giz.de/sites/default/files/media/els-document/2026-06/ruta-conformidad-productores-cacao-eudr-compressed_0.pdf)

#### (a) Land use rights

**Colombian legal sources** (from the EFI Annex 2 mapping unless marked otherwise):
- Constitución 1991, arts. 58 and 64.
- Código Civil (Ley 57 de 1887): property, possession (arts. 762–828), lease, comodato, usufruct.
- Ley 1564 de 2012 (Código General del Proceso).
- Ley 1561 de 2012: special titling procedure for possessors of small properties.
- Ley 160 de 1994: agrarian reform and baldíos.
- Decreto Ley 902 de 2017, arts. 36–37: ANT titles possession by administrative act.
- Ley 1776 de 2016 (ZIDRES). Its art. 4 makes MinAgricultura responsible for defining the agricultural frontier.
- Res. 261 de 2018 (MinAgricultura): Frontera Agrícola Nacional, modified by Res. 327 de 2025.
- Ley 6 de 1975 (aparcería).
- Ley 1448 de 2011 (víctimas y restitución; RTDAF).
- Ley 387 de 1997 (RUPTA).
- Decreto 1071 de 2015 (sector agropecuario).
- Ley 388 de 1997 (POT/EOT) and Decreto 1077 de 2015.
- Ley 1801 de 2016 (police protection of possession).

**Practical evidence** (any one is enough, per Colcocoa):
- Escritura pública.
- INCORA/INCODER/ANT adjudication resolution for a baldío. Documents from the old agencies "remain valid"; if lost, request a copy from the ANT.
- Certificado de tradición y libertad. Obtained from the SNR online with the matrícula inmobiliaria, costs about COP 21,900, and should be no older than 6 months.
- Lease contract, or a sworn declaration if the contract is verbal.
- Purchase contract authenticated at a notary.
- Impuesto predial invoice.
- Certificado de sana posesión: a self-declaration witnessed by the president of the JAC is enough, or a document from the Alcaldía.
- Plus: a SIPRA/UPRA Frontera Agrícola overlay. If the plot is in a conditioned area, a municipal certificado de uso del suelo.

**Checklist question:** "Which document shows your right to farm this plot (title, adjudication, lease, notarised purchase, predial bill, or JAC-witnessed sana posesión)? Is the plot inside the Frontera Agrícola?"

**Red flags:**
- Plot overlaps the RTDAF/URT restitution register, or abandoned land registered in RUPTA.
- ANT complaints about irregular occupation.
- Plot is outside or conditioned by the Frontera Agrícola.
- Claimant names on the certificado de tradición do not match the producer's cédula.
- Plot lies in a resguardo or community council territory but the farmer is not a member.

#### (b) Environmental protection

**Colombian legal sources:**
- Decreto-Ley 2811 de 1974 (Código de Recursos Naturales).
- Ley 99 de 1993 (SINA, CARs).
- Decreto 1076 de 2015 (DUR Ambiente): SINAP, water concessions, discharges, soil art. 2.2.1.1.18.6, burning art. 2.2.5.1.3.14.
- Decreto 622 de 1977 (national parks).
- Decreto 3600 de 2007.
- Ley 357 de 1997 (Ramsar) and Ley 1753 de 2015, art. 172.
- Ley 1930 de 2018 (páramos).
- Ley 1955 de 2019, art. 279: rural dispersed dwellings are exempt from water concession and discharge permits for domestic use.
- Res. 330 de 2017: VRD still need registration with the CAR (cited by Colcocoa).
- Decreto 1449 de 1977: 30 m protective strip along water bodies.
- Agrochemicals: Ley 9 de 1979, Decreto 1843 de 1991, ICA resolutions, Ley 1650 de 2013.
- Sanctions: Ley 1333 de 2009 and Ley 2387 de 2024.
- Note: Decreto 1076, art. 2.2.2.3.2.1 does **not** require an environmental licence for coffee, cocoa or palm cultivation.

**Practical evidence** (per Colcocoa):
- CAR water concession and discharge permit; **or**
- vereda or municipal aqueduct bill; **or**
- irrigation district certificate; **or**
- registration as a water-resource user with the CAR (free, for VRD); **or**
- self-declaration of no water use, backed by an inspection.
- GIZ also suggests a SIPRA check showing the plot is not in a protection zone.

**Checklist question:** "Where does the farm's water come from? Do you have an aqueduct bill, a CAR concession or a CAR user registration? Is the plot outside parks, páramos and Ramsar sites?"

**Red flags:**
- Overlap with RUNAP, páramo, a Ramsar site or a Ley 2 Zone A.
- Open burning without a permit.
- Banned pesticides.
- Cultivation inside the 30 m stream buffer. EFI rates this as "no pertinente" because rondas are not yet delimited.
- Open sanctions proceedings at the CAR.

#### (c) Forest-related rules (forest management; biodiversity where directly related to wood harvesting)

**Colombian legal sources:**
- Ley 2 de 1959: 7 national forest reserves, zoned A (strict), B and C.
- Res. 110 de 2022 (MinAmbiente): sustracción (removal of land from a forest reserve).
- Decreto 1076 de 2015, forest harvesting rules: aprovechamiento único, isolated trees, and registration of shade trees and agroforestry plantations (arts. 2.2.1.1.12.2–3).
- Decreto 2398 de 2019 (Ventanilla Única Forestal).
- Res. 1909 de 2017 and 081 de 2018 (SUNL forest transport permit).
- Ley 2111 de 2021: deforestation became a crime (Código Penal art. 330) from 29 Jul 2021.
- Ley 160 de 1994, art. 52: agrarian extinction of ownership for breaching the ecological function of property.
- Decreto 1608 de 1978 (wildlife), Res. 1912 de 2017 (threatened species), Ley 165 de 1994 (Convention on Biological Diversity).

**Practical evidence:**
- A GIS check that the plot is not in dry tropical forest, a biosphere reserve, a Ley 2 reserve, a resguardo, a wetland or a páramo.
- If primary or secondary forest was cleared, the forest harvesting permit from the CAR (Colcocoa).
- Note: shade trees in coffee and cocoa systems that are later cut or sold may need registration or a salvoconducto. **verify** whether this applies to smallholders.

**Checklist question:** "Was any tree or forest cleared on this plot since 2020? If yes, which CAR permit covers it? Is the plot inside a Ley 2 de 1959 reserve, and in which zone?"

**Red flags:**
- Forest-loss alerts after 31 Dec 2020.
- Ley 2 Zone A.
- Cleared with no permit.
- Open Ley 2111 or Ley 1333 proceedings.

#### (d) Third parties' rights

**Colombian legal sources:**
- Constitución arts. 7, 8, 63, 79 and 330.
- Ley 70 de 1993: collective titles of Black communities, under consejos comunitarios.
- Ley 160 de 1994, art. 85 (resguardos), Decreto 2001 de 1998, Decreto 2333 de 2014.
- Decretos-Ley 4633, 4634 and 4635 de 2011 (ethnic victims).
- Ley 1448 de 2011.
- Decreto 1500 de 2018 (Línea Negra, Sierra Nevada; relevant to coffee).
- Cultural heritage: Ley 1185 de 2008, Ley 45 de 1983.
- Contracts on collective territories need community approval (Ley 70 and Decreto 1071).

**Practical evidence:**
- Overlay against resguardos, community council territories, Ley 70 reserve areas and the restitution registers.
- If the plot lies in an ethnic territory, a certificado de tradición showing it is not resguardo land (Colcocoa). Otherwise, authorisation from the cabildo or consejo comunitario. **verify** the exact form of that authorisation.
- Searches of NGO and press reports on land conflicts (EFI, GIZ).
- Colcocoa also applies a UPRA aptitude check and asks for a municipal land-use certificate in conditioned areas.

**Checklist question:** "Does the plot overlap an indigenous resguardo, an Afro-Colombian collective title or a land restitution claim? If yes, what authorisation does the community give?"

**Red flags:**
- Overlap with a resguardo or collective title and no community authorisation.
- A URT restitution claim.
- Reports of displacement or dispossession in the vereda.

#### (e) Labour rights

**Colombian legal sources:**
- Código Sustantivo del Trabajo: contracts (verbal or written; fixed-term must be written, art. 46), working hours arts. 160–161, minimum wage, unions arts. 359 and 373 (minimum 25 workers).
- Ley 2101 de 2021: working week cut gradually from 48 h to 42 h.
- Ley 2466 de 2025: labour reform.
- Ley 789 de 2002.
- Ley 100 de 1993 (social security), Ley 1562 de 2012 (occupational risk), Decreto 1072 de 2015 (DUR Trabajo, SG-SST), Res. 0312 de 2019 (SG-SST minimum standards, including small agricultural producers).
- Non-discrimination and harassment: Ley 1010 de 2006, Ley 2365 de 2024, Ley 1496 de 2011, Ley 1257 de 2008.
- Res. 464 de 2017 (MinAgricultura): family, peasant and community farming (ACFC).
- Ley 2114 de 2021: parental leave.

**Practical evidence:**
- About 90% of cocoa families rely on family labour (Colcocoa), and over 76% of coffee farms are family-based (FNC).
- Declaration of family labour, verified through a voluntary standard (e.g. Echar Pa'lante).
- If external workers are hired: written or verbal contract, pay at or above minimum wage, at most 44 h/week (Colcocoa's figure), payment in kind at most 30%, PPE, social-security affiliation (PILA). **verify** PILA for seasonal pickers.
- GIZ also lists health and safety documents, contracts and worker interviews.

**Checklist question:** "Does the farm hire non-family workers, including seasonal pickers? If yes, are they paid at least the minimum wage and given PPE?"

**Red flags:**
- Seasonal harvest labour that does not meet national requirements. The FNC itself says this happens often.
- No PPE for workers applying agrochemicals.
- Labour intermediation (tercerización).

#### (f) Human rights protected under international law

**Colombian legal sources:**
- Constitución, fundamental rights.
- ILO Conventions 138 and 182 (child labour) and 29 and 105 (forced labour), the latter approved by Ley 23 de 1967 / Decreto 1280 de 1997.
- Ley 985 de 2005 (trafficking).
- Ley 1098 de 2006 (Código de Infancia).
- Res. 3597 de 2013: minimum working age 15, with MinTrabajo authorisation.
- Decreto 1174 de 2016 and **Res. 1796 de 2018** (MinTrabajo): hazardous work prohibited for minors, cited by EFI. **verify** the exact text of Res. 1796 for agrochemical and harvesting tasks.
- No night work for minors.

**Practical evidence:**
- Producer declaration of zero tolerance for child and forced labour.
- Proof that children attend school (Colcocoa).
- Query to ICBF for child-labour reports in the sourcing area (EFI).
- Anonymous grievance channel.
- For forced labour: confirm workers keep their documents and are free to leave.

**Checklist question:** "Do any under-18s work on the farm? If 15–17, do they have MinTrabajo authorisation and no hazardous tasks? Are all workers free to leave and paid in full?"

**Red flags:**
- Minors under 15 working.
- Minors applying agrochemicals.
- Debt bondage, or ID documents withheld.
- Armed-actor presence or coca in the vereda.

#### (g) FPIC, including UNDRIP

**Colombian legal sources:**
- Convenio 169 OIT, approved by **Ley 21 de 1991**.
- Ley 70 de 1993 (extends consultation to Afro-Colombian communities).
- Decreto 1320 de 1998 (consulta previa procedure).
- Decreto 2613 de 2016 (protocol).
- Decreto 2353 de 2019 (Dirección/Subdirección de Consulta Previa, Mininterior).
- Ley 2294 de 2023 (PND 2022–26).
- Directiva Presidencial 2013.
- Constitutional Court case law (T-129/2011 standard). **verify**; not in the sources read.
- EFI distinguishes consulta previa (a state obligation for projects) from CLPI/FPIC (a private-sector good practice).

**Practical evidence:**
- Only triggered if the plot is in or next to an ethnic territory.
- Minutes or records of consultation or consent with the cabildo or consejo comunitario (GIZ).
- For indigenous producers themselves: membership and endorsement by the cabildo, plus the certificado de tradición showing resguardo status (Colcocoa).

**Checklist question:** "Is the plot inside or bordering an indigenous or Afro-Colombian territory? If yes, is there a record of the community's free, prior and informed consent?"

**Red flags:**
- Expansion into resguardo or community council land without consent.
- Complaints from ethnic authorities.
- Línea Negra overlap (Sierra Nevada coffee).

#### (h) Tax, anti-corruption, trade and customs

**Colombian legal sources:**
- Estatuto Tributario (Decreto 624 de 1989): RUT; renta, where most low-income smallholders are not obliged to file; IVA arts. 420–421.
- ET art. 583: tax secrecy, so a self-declaration of tax compliance is acceptable.
- Impuesto predial: Ley 44 de 1990. EFI rates it "no pertinente".
- ICA: Ley 14 de 1983. Applies to cooperatives and traders, not to farming. EFI rates it "no pertinente".
- Parafiscales (Ley 101 de 1993): Contribución Cafetera (Ley 9 de 1991) and Cuota de Fomento Cacaotero (Ley 67 de 1983). **verify** these two law numbers; EFI says parafiscales apply to coffee, cocoa and palm.
- Customs: Decreto 1165 de 2019, Decreto-Ley 920 de 2023.
- Anti-corruption: Ley 1474 de 2011 and Ley 2195 de 2022.
- Factura electrónica (DIAN): an obligation for the cooperative or exporter. Natural-person farmers below DIAN thresholds are generally not obliged; buyers issue a documento soporte. **verify**.
- Ley 1581 de 2012 (personal data; not in Art 2(40)): needed for the app's handling of cédulas and coordinates. FNC field staff collect written consent before sharing SICA data ([Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/03/colombias-coffee-industry-well-placed-but-wary-as-eu-deforestation-rule-looms/)).

**Practical evidence:**
- Producer: RUT, free from DIAN online or in person; latest renta return, or a self-declaration of tax compliance if not obliged (Colcocoa).
- Cooperative or exporter: RUT, Cámara de Comercio certificate, export declaration (DEX), paz y salvo for parafiscales, anti-corruption policy, e-invoices (EFI, GIZ).

**Checklist question:** "Does the producer have a RUT? Are they obliged to file renta, and if so is it filed? Has the exporter paid the contribución cafetera / cuota cacaotera and filed the export declaration?"

**Red flags:**
- No RUT.
- Cash purchases through unregistered intermediaries.
- Missing export declarations.
- A cooperative not registered or in Supersolidaria intervention. **verify** whether Supersolidaria intervention lists are published.

### Inferences
- **One evidence item per category.** For the MVP, each category can be a single evidence slot plus one geospatial auto-check. Category (a) takes a document. Categories (b), (c), (d) and (g) are driven mostly by GIS overlays (UPRA Frontera Agrícola via SIPRA, Ley 2 zoning, RUNAP, resguardos and community councils from ANT, the URT register). Categories (e), (f) and (h) are mostly producer declarations plus a RUT.
- **Risk-gated depth.** Because the Commission guidance says negligible-risk supply chains need no per-plot titles, the app should treat documents as escalation items. Request them only when a GIS overlay or a red flag fires; do not make them mandatory for every plot.
- **Coffee vs palm.** EFI's palm-specific risk ratings (e.g. 70–95.5% of palm growers hold titles) should not be copied to coffee. Coffee and cocoa informality is higher. Use the Cenicafé coffee tool once it can be read.

## Q3. How cooperatives handle informality: sana posesión, Ley 2 reserve zones, baldíos

### Takeaway
Informal tenure is normal and is not illegal in Colombian law. Cooperatives accept several kinds of proof:
- notarised purchase contracts;
- predial bills;
- a JAC-witnessed sana posesión declaration;
- INCORA/INCODER adjudication papers;
- verbal leases backed by a sworn declaration.

Ley 2 zones B and C, and baldíos, are "conditioned" rather than prohibited. They should be flagged for review, not rejected automatically.

### Cited Findings
- The legal basis for informal possession:
  - Possession is a protected right: Código Civil arts. 762–828; Ley 1776 de 2016.
  - Ley 1561 de 2012: titling procedure for small possessors.
  - Decreto Ley 902 de 2017: ANT titling by administrative act.
  - EFI accepts "informal title" (unregistered sale or unformalised inheritance) and "regular possession with proof of occupation and improvements". Verbal contracts can be verified through minutes, neighbours, associations or cooperatives.

  — [EFI palm tool](https://fedepalma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ANO-2026-FINAL-Guia-legalidad-palma-aceite-EUDR_Colombia.pdf)
- Colcocoa: a self-declaration of sana tenencia signed by the JAC president as witness "is sufficient". INCORA and INCODER baldío adjudications remain valid, and the ANT reissues them. — [Colcocoa ruta](https://www.giz.de/sites/default/files/media/els-document/2026-06/ruta-conformidad-productores-cacao-eudr-compressed_0.pdf)
- How the UPRA Frontera Agrícola map classifies zones:
  - **Legal exclusions:** páramos, national and regional parks, **Ley 2 Zone A**, protective forest reserves, archaeological areas, La Macarena preservation zones.
  - **Conditioned:** Black-community lands, Ley 70 reserve areas, resguardos, **Ley 2 zones B, C and uncategorised**, DMI/DRMI, RNSC, Ramsar sites, mangroves, biosphere reserves, AICAs.
  - Plots outside or near the frontier line: request a municipal certificado de uso del suelo.

  — [EFI palm tool, Cat. 1–2](https://fedepalma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ANO-2026-FINAL-Guia-legalidad-palma-aceite-EUDR_Colombia.pdf)
- Baldíos inside reserves or other non-adjudicable areas are governed by Ley 160 de 1994, Decreto 902 de 2017 and ANT agreements. Sustracción from Ley 2 reserves is under Res. 110 de 2022. — [EFI palm tool](https://fedepalma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ANO-2026-FINAL-Guia-legalidad-palma-aceite-EUDR_Colombia.pdf)
- FNC: Colombia has "a certain level of informality, especially in rural areas", and unclear tenure is a major legal challenge. ### Inferences
- Suggested app status logic:
  - **Green:** registered title, adjudication or certificado de tradición, **and** inside the Frontera Agrícola.
  - **Amber (accepted with note):** sana posesión via JAC, notarised purchase, lease or predial bill, **or** plot in Ley 2 Zone B/C or another conditioned area. Ask for a municipal land-use certificate, and confirm no forest was cleared after 2020.
  - **Red (escalate):** Ley 2 Zone A, a park or páramo, an ethnic territory without community endorsement, or a restitution claim.
- An occupied baldío without adjudication should be Amber, not Red. Possession is legally protected and can be titled by the ANT. It turns Red if the baldío is non-adjudicable (inside a reserve or park).
