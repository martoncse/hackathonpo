## Q1. Who must file what after Regulation 2025/2650, and what changed from the 2023 text

### Takeaway
The DDS is now filed only by the upstream "operator", the first entity to place on the EU market or export. Downstream operators and traders no longer file DDS. They passively collect and keep supplier and customer details, plus the DDS reference number when their supplier is an operator. The new one-off simplified declaration (SD) is available only to micro and small primary operators (MSPOs) established in a low-risk country. Colombia is standard risk, so a Colombian coffee or cocoa cooperative or farmer cannot use the SD. For Colombian supply, the EU importer (or a non-EU importer of record) files a full DDS after full due diligence (Art. 9, 10 and 11), and EUDR-Snap's job is to feed that DDS and the due diligence file behind it.

### Cited Findings
**Roles and definitions (as amended)**
- Art. 2(15) "operator" now expressly excludes downstream operators. New Art. 2(15a) defines an MSPO: a natural person or micro/small undertaking, established in a low-risk country, placing on the market or exporting relevant products it produced itself. New Art. 2(15b) defines a downstream operator: one that places on the market or exports products made using relevant products already covered by a DDS or SD. Art. 2(17) "trader" is now any person in the supply chain other than the operator or downstream operator. Art. 2(30): SME status applies "irrespective of their legal form". — [Reg. 2025/2650, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)
- MSPO requires all four of: (a) natural person or micro/small undertaking under the Accounting Directive thresholds; (b) established in a low-risk country; (c) directly placing on the EU market or exporting; (d) products it grew, harvested or raised itself. "Primary producers established outside of the EU are not covered by this definition if they supply products to intermediaries outside of the EU", and such producers "have no legal obligations under the Regulation". — [FAQ v5, Q3.21](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- Art. 3(c) now reads "covered by a due diligence statement or a simplified declaration". — [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)

**Operators (Art. 4)**
- Unchanged core: due diligence before placing or exporting (Art. 4(1)); DDS submitted before placing or exporting (Art. 4(2)); responsibility assumed and DDS records kept for 5 years from submission (Art. 4(3)); no placing where non-compliant, where there is non-negligible risk, or where the operator could not fulfil its obligations (Art. 4(4)). — [EUDR original text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115)
- Amended Art. 4(7): operators pass downstream "reference numbers of the due diligence statements or, if applicable, the declaration identifiers". The old wording, which required passing "all information necessary to demonstrate that due diligence was exercised", is gone. Art. 4(8) to 4(10) are deleted. These were the SME-operator exemption and the non-SME rule for referencing earlier DDS. — [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng); [original Art. 4(7)-(10)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115)
- FAQ: there is "no legal obligation on operators to provide additional information which would enable downstream operators or traders to exercise due diligence", and no obligation to share verification numbers downstream. — [FAQ v5, Q3.6.1](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)

**MSPO simplified declaration (new Art. 4a)**
- Art. 4a(1) exempts MSPOs from Art. 4(2) (the DDS), the second sentence of Art. 4(3), and Art. 4(4)(c). Art. 4a(2): a one-time SD before first placing or export, after which the system issues a declaration identifier. Art. 4a(3): the content is in Annex III, and the SD may be updated after major changes. Art. 4a(4): no SD is needed where the Annex III data already sits in a Union or national system. Art. 4a(5): geolocation may be replaced by a postal address. — [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)
- Annex III (SD content): (1) operator name, address and EORI where applicable; (2) HS code, description, trade name and estimated annual quantity; (3) country of production and geolocation or postal address of all plots/establishments; (4) a standard due diligence confirmation statement. — [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng). Exact wording of the Annex III statement: **verify** in the OJ text.
- The postal address may be standard address fields or "cadastral information or an equivalent". An administrative-only business seat cannot be declared. — [FAQ v5, Q3.28](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- An SD need not be updated for quantity changes, but new products (a new HS code) need a new or updated SD. One SD may cover several products and plots. — [FAQ v5, Q3.27, Q3.29](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- MSPOs "source, by definition, from low-risk countries and are, therefore, never required to carry out risk assessment or risk mitigation unless a risk of non-compliance is identified". — [COM(2026) 191, s. 4.1.3](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a3c5c3a0-232e-43c4-b0b8-1eecb1df45c7_en?filename=Report+from+the+Commission+to+the+Council+and+Parliament+on+the+EUDR.pdf)

**Downstream operators and traders (new Art. 5)**
- Art. 5(1): they hold the required information before placing or making available. Art. 5(2): non-SMEs register in the Information System. Art. 5(4): records kept for at least 5 years. Art. 5(6): non-SMEs verify that due diligence was done when there are substantiated concerns. — [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)
- Information to collect: name, registered trade name or mark, postal address, email and (if available) web address of direct suppliers and direct customers. The "first downstream operator or trader", whose supplier is the upstream operator, also keeps the DDS reference numbers or SD identifiers. It does not need to pass them on, actively seek them, or check their validity systematically, and it links them to incoming flows only. — [FAQ v5, Q3.4, Q3.5](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf); [Green Forum](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/deforestation-regulation-implementation/roles-and-responsibilities_en)
- All downstream operators and traders, SME or not, must notify competent authorities and their customers of new information indicating risk (Art. 5(5)). Under Art. 5(6), the non-SME verification duty is reactive only. — [FAQ v5, Q3.6.2](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- The Commission describes the downstream role as "limited to passive collection and retention of relevant information". — [COM(2026) 191](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a3c5c3a0-232e-43c4-b0b8-1eecb1df45c7_en?filename=Report+from+the+Commission+to+the+Council+and+Parliament+on+the+EUDR.pdf)

**Non-EU actors, cooperatives, authorised representatives**
- A non-EU importer releasing goods for free circulation is an upstream operator and needs an EU EORI. Under Art. 7, the first EU-established person making the product available is also deemed an operator and files its own DDS, so a chain can have two operators. Non-EU smallholders selling to buyers who do the importing "have no direct EUDR obligations", although buyers may ask them for geolocation. — [Green Forum](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/deforestation-regulation-implementation/roles-and-responsibilities_en); [FAQ v5, Q3.7](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- A cooperative files in its own name only if it is itself the operator, meaning it places on the EU market. It qualifies as an MSPO only if it meets all elements of Art. 2(15a), including producing the products itself. A cooperative acting as authorised representative (Art. 6) "must be established in the EU". — [FAQ v5, Q3.20, Q3.22](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- New Art. 6(3): a natural person or microenterprise may mandate the next downstream operator or trader that is not a natural person or microenterprise to file for it. — [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)

**Dates**
- Art. 38(2): Arts. 3 to 13, 16 to 24, 26, 31 and 32 apply from **30 December 2026**. Art. 38(3): **30 June 2027** for operators that were micro or small undertakings (or natural persons) established by 31 Dec 2024. Micro/small enterprises previously covered by the EUTR (timber) start on 30 Dec 2026. — [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng); [Green Forum](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/deforestation-regulation-implementation/roles-and-responsibilities_en)
- Reg. 2024/3234 only amended the date-of-application provisions. Its dates (30 Dec 2025 and 30 June 2026) are **superseded** by 2025/2650. — [COM(2026) 191, fn 30](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a3c5c3a0-232e-43c4-b0b8-1eecb1df45c7_en?filename=Report+from+the+Commission+to+the+Council+and+Parliament+on+the+EUDR.pdf)
- Stibbe flags an open issue: from 30 Dec 2026 to 30 June 2027, downstream actors must collect identifiers that micro and small operators do not yet have to issue. — [Stibbe](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/the-amended-eudr-what-has-changed-and-what-has-remained)

**2026 review outcome**
- Art. 34(1a) required a simplification review by 30 April 2026. COM(2026) 191 was published on 4 May 2026 and concludes that the Commission "does not consider it appropriate to propose further amendments to the basic legal text". It acts through updated Guidance and FAQ, a delegated act on scope, Information System updates (reopening June 2026, SD submission, new roles, APIs, voluntary grouping of DDS reference numbers) and repositories of producer-country legislation and certification schemes "by end of 2026". It estimates a cost cut of about 75% (EUR 8.1bn to 2.0bn a year; this is a Commission estimate). Stakeholder proposals for a "zero-risk" country category were not adopted. — [COM(2026) 191](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a3c5c3a0-232e-43c4-b0b8-1eecb1df45c7_en?filename=Report+from+the+Commission+to+the+Council+and+Parliament+on+the+EUDR.pdf)
- The next general review is due by 30 June 2030 (Art. 34(2)). — [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)

### Inferences
- In the typical Colombian chain (farmer, then cooperative/exporter, then EU importer), the EU importer is the operator and files the DDS. The cooperative has no direct legal filing duty, but it is the de facto data supplier. EUDR-Snap's outputs are therefore a **DDS-ready data package** for the importer, plus the **due diligence evidence file** the importer must keep and be able to show. They are not a filing by the cooperative itself.
- If the cooperative or exporter is itself the importer of record (it holds an EU EORI and releases goods for free circulation), it becomes an operator and must file a full DDS. Colombia's standard risk status means no SD.
- The SD module in EUDR-Snap is only relevant if the app expands to low-risk origins (for example Ghana or Vietnam, which are on the low-risk list) and to producers who export directly.

### Gaps
- Exact wording of the Annex III standard statement, and the precise amended Art. 9(1)(b) text on supplementary units for SDs: **verify** against the OJ text of 2025/2650.
- The Implementing Regulation 2024/3084 amendment on Information System functions (SD format, grouping) was announced, but its final adoption and number were not confirmed: **verify**.

## Q2. DDS content (Annex II), Art. 9 information requirements, geolocation rules, Art. 2(40) legislation list

### Takeaway
A DDS has five fields: operator identity and EORI; HS code, description and quantity; country of production and geolocation; a fixed compliance sentence; and a signature block. (Point 4, which referenced earlier DDS, was deleted by 2025/2650.) The due diligence file behind it (Art. 9) must also hold production dates, supplier and customer contacts, and "adequately conclusive and verifiable" deforestation-free and legality evidence, kept for 5 years.

### Cited Findings
**Annex II DDS (original text; point 4 now deleted)**
1. Operator name, address and, for products entering or leaving the market, EORI number (Art. 9 of Reg. 952/2013).
2. HS code, free-text description including trade name (and full scientific name where applicable), and quantity. For import or export, quantity is in kg net mass plus the supplementary unit where defined; otherwise net mass "specifying a percentage estimate or deviation", volume or number of items.
3. Country of production and geolocation of all plots of land (for cattle, all establishments). Multiple plots must all be included.
4. ~~Reference number of an existing DDS (Art. 4(8)-(9))~~. **Deleted** by 2025/2650.
5. The text: "By submitting this due diligence statement the operator confirms that due diligence in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2023/1115 was carried out and that no or only a negligible risk was found that the relevant products do not comply with Article 3, point (a) or (b), of that Regulation."
6. Signature: "Signed for and on behalf of: / Date: / Name and function: Signature:".
— [EUDR Annex II](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115); deletion of point 4: [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)

**Art. 9(1), information kept for 5 years "accompanied by evidence", per relevant product**
- (a) description, trade name and type; for wood, the common and full scientific species name; the list of relevant commodities and products contained.
- (b) quantity (kg net mass plus supplementary unit for import or export; otherwise net mass, volume or number of items). Amended by 2025/2650 for SD supplementary units (**verify** wording).
- (c) country of production and, where relevant, parts thereof.
- (d) geolocation of all plots plus the "date or time range of production". Any deforestation or degradation on a plot automatically disqualifies everything from that plot.
- (e) name, postal address and email of suppliers.
- (f) name, postal address and email of customers (as amended: any business, downstream operator or trader supplied).
- (g) "adequately conclusive and verifiable information that the relevant products are deforestation-free".
- (h) "adequately conclusive and verifiable information" of production in accordance with the relevant legislation of the country of production, "including any arrangement conferring the right to use the respective area".
- Art. 9(2): this information must be made available to competent authorities on request.
— [EUDR Art. 9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115); [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)
- Date of production means the harvest date. If more precise data is unavailable, "the crop year and/or harvesting season could be used". — [FAQ v5, Q1.25](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)

**Geolocation**
- Art. 2(28): latitude and longitude "using at least six decimal digits". For plots of more than 4 ha used for commodities other than cattle, polygons "with sufficient latitude and longitude points to describe the perimeter of each plot of land". — [EUDR Art. 2(28)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115)
- Plots under 4 ha may use a single point or a polygon. Cattle establishments use a single point. No circle-plus-radius format is allowed. One polygon equals one plot; several plots need several polygons in the same DDS. — [FAQ v5, Q1.1, Q1.8, Q1.14, Q1.16](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- Art. 2(27) "plot of land": land within a single real-estate property, "as recognised by the law of the country of production", homogeneous enough for risk evaluation. It can be the whole property or only the cultivated part. Illegality anywhere on the property (for example illegal clearing elsewhere on it) taints the declared part. — [FAQ v5, Q1.15](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- No land title or registry is needed to geolocate. "If possession of a land title is not required under domestic law to produce and commercialise agricultural products, then it is not required under the Regulation." The operator is responsible for accuracy even when data comes via cooperatives. — [FAQ v5, Q1.10, Q1.11, Q1.12](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- Geolocation is required even for low-risk origins. — [FAQ v5, Q1.28](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- Information System technical limits: GeoJSON only, WGS-84 / EPSG:4326. Maximum 25 MB per DDS (about 1 million vertices). 200 product lines per DDS, 1,000 production places per line, 10,000 production places per DDS, 50 drafts per user. Coordinates with fewer than 6 decimals are zero-padded automatically. Each country of production is entered separately. — [FAQ v5, Q7.8, Q7.12, Q7.13, Q7.16, Q7.17, Q7.26](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- HS codes must be declared to at least the digit level used in Annex I (up to 6 digits allowed). Net mass excludes packaging. — [FAQ v5, Q7.22, Q7.24](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)

**Art. 2(40) "relevant legislation of the country of production"**: laws "concerning the legal status of the area of production" in terms of:
- (a) land use rights;
- (b) environmental protection;
- (c) forest-related rules, including forest management and biodiversity conservation, where directly related to wood harvesting;
- (d) third parties' rights;
- (e) labour rights;
- (f) human rights protected under international law;
- (g) FPIC, including as set out in UNDRIP;
- (h) tax, anti-corruption, trade and customs regulations.
— [EUDR Art. 2(40)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115)
- Only the laws of the country of production apply, not those of processing countries. Evidence may be "official documents from public authorities, contractual agreements, court decisions or impact assessments and audits", and it must be verifiable and reliable given the corruption risk. — [FAQ v5, Q3.3](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- EUDR-Snap should generate, for each lot or shipment: (1) a GeoJSON FeatureCollection, with one Feature per plot (Point if under 4 ha, Polygon otherwise), 6 or more decimals, WGS-84, with properties such as producer name, plot ID, area in ha and harvest period; (2) an Annex II field sheet (HS code 0901 for coffee or 1801 for cocoa beans, description, net kg, country CO); (3) an Art. 9 dossier with supplier and customer contacts, deforestation-free evidence (for example a satellite overlay against the 2020 forest baseline) and legality evidence indexed to Art. 2(40)(a)-(h).
- Polygon file sizes are unlikely to be a constraint for smallholder coffee. The limit of 10,000 production places per DDS matters for large cooperative lots.

### Gaps
- Exact Colombian legal instruments for each Art. 2(40) letter were not researched here. The Commission's legislation repository was planned for end-2026 and no confirmed launch was found: **verify**. An EU-supported EFI legal study covers Colombian cocoa and coffee: [EFI methodological note](https://efi.int/sites/default/files/files/flegtredd/Sustainable-cocoa-programme/Documents/Methodological%20note%20for%20legal%20studies.pdf) (not reviewed in detail).

## Q3. Art. 10 risk assessment, Art. 11 mitigation, Art. 12 due diligence system and retention, Art. 13 simplified due diligence

### Takeaway
For standard-risk Colombia, the operator must run a documented Art. 10 assessment against criteria (a)-(n), mitigate any residual risk to "negligible" under Art. 11, review both annually, and keep everything for 5 years. Art. 13 simplified due diligence does not apply, because it requires all products to come from low-risk countries.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 10(1): verify and analyse the Art. 9 information. No placing unless the assessment shows "no or only a negligible risk". — [EUDR Art. 10](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115)
- Art. 10(2) criteria (full list):
  - (a) Art. 29 risk level of the country or part thereof;
  - (b) presence of forests;
  - (c) presence of indigenous peoples;
  - (d) consultation and cooperation in good faith with indigenous peoples;
  - (e) duly reasoned claims by indigenous peoples on use or ownership of the area;
  - (f) prevalence of deforestation or forest degradation;
  - (g) source, reliability, validity and links to other documentation of the Art. 9(1) information;
  - (h) country concerns: corruption, document and data falsification, lack of law enforcement, human rights violations, armed conflict, UN or EU sanctions;
  - (i) supply chain complexity and stage of processing, in particular difficulty linking products to plots;
  - (j) risk of circumvention or mixing with products of unknown origin or from deforested areas;
  - (k) conclusions of Commission expert groups;
  - (l) substantiated concerns (Art. 31) and history of non-compliance in the chain;
  - (m) any information pointing to non-compliance risk;
  - (n) complementary information, including from certification or third-party schemes, provided it meets Art. 9 requirements.
  — [EUDR Art. 10(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115)
- Art. 10(4): document and review risk assessments at least annually, and be able to "demonstrate how the information gathered was checked against the risk assessment criteria ... and how they determined the degree of risk". — [EUDR Art. 10(4)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115)
- Art. 11(1) mitigation measures: (a) requiring additional information, data or documents; (b) independent surveys or audits; (c) other measures on the Art. 9 information; plus support to suppliers, "in particular smallholders, through capacity building and investments". Art. 11(2): policies, controls and procedures, including a compliance officer at management level and an independent audit function (both for non-SMEs). Art. 11(3): mitigation decisions documented, reviewed annually and demonstrable. — [EUDR Art. 11](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115)
- Art. 12: a due diligence system is established, reviewed "at least once a year", and records of its updates are kept for 5 years (12(2)). Non-SME operators publish an annual public report (12(3)-(4)): a summary of the Art. 9(1)(a)-(c) data, risk conclusions and measures, and any consultation of indigenous peoples and communities. The first report covers 2027 and is published after 30 Dec 2027. All due diligence documentation is kept for at least 5 years (12(5)). Arts. 12 and 13 were not amended by 2025/2650. — [EUDR Art. 12](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115); [FAQ v5, Q5.14](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf); [Reg. 2025/2650 analysis](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)
- Retention periods: Art. 9 information is kept 5 years from placing or export; risk and mitigation documentation 5 years after the due diligence exercise; the DDS 5 years from submission. — [FAQ v5, Q5.8](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- Art. 13(1): Arts. 10 and 11 are not required only where the operator has ascertained that **all** products were produced in low-risk countries, after assessing complexity and circumvention or mixing risk. Documentation of negligible circumvention or mixing risk must still be available. Art. 13(2) reverts to the full regime if risk information emerges. — [EUDR Art. 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115)
- "Due diligence is not a 'tick-the-box exercise'". The Commission provides no pre-set format or questionnaire. — [FAQ v5, Q5.16](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- A single DDS can cover multiple shipments, but should not cover more than one year from submission (linked to the Art. 12(2) annual review). Quantities must match, and any unshipped difference must be explained in records kept 5 years. A DDS can be filed before harvest only for stable plots. — [FAQ v5, Q5.19](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- The app's "risk assessment report" generator should mirror Art. 10(2)(a)-(n) one criterion at a time, recording the evidence checked, the finding and the risk rating. A mitigation log should mirror Art. 11(1)(a)-(c). Both should be timestamped with an annual review date, to satisfy Art. 10(4) and 11(3).
- Colombia-relevant criteria include (c)-(e) (indigenous and Afro-Colombian territories) and (h) (armed conflict, corruption). Expect these to be scrutinised.

### Gaps
- No official Commission model risk assessment template exists (FAQ Q5.16), so the generator must be designed, not copied.

## Q4. Country benchmarking: Colombia's risk level and its consequences

### Takeaway
Colombia does not appear on either list in Implementing Regulation 2025/1093, so it is **standard risk**. The consequences: competent-authority checks cover at least 3% of relevant operators (versus 1% for low-risk origins), simplified due diligence under Art. 13 is not available, and Colombian producers cannot qualify as MSPOs.

### Cited Findings
- The 2025/1093 Annex lists low-risk countries (including all EU Member States, the US, Ghana, Vietnam, Uruguay, Chile, Costa Rica and Papua New Guinea) and high-risk countries (Belarus, DPRK, Myanmar, Russia). Colombia appears on neither list. — [Implementing Reg. 2025/1093, Annex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R1093)
- Unlisted countries are standard risk, and Colombia is named as standard risk by law-firm summaries. — [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102kcln/european-commission-sets-country-risk-classifications-for-eu-deforestation-regula)
- Amended Art. 16: annual checks cover at least 3% (standard), 9% (high) and 1% (low risk) of relevant operators and of non-SME downstream operators and traders. Checks happen without prior warning (Art. 16(13)). — [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)
- A first review of the benchmarking, using FAO FRA 2025 data, was "envisaged" for 2026. The Parliament voted to object to 2025/1093 (373 to 289), but the Commission kept it in place. — [EP resolution TA-10-2025-0149](https://data.europarl.europa.eu/distribution/doc/TA-10-2025-0149_en.pdf); [Green Forum cooperation page](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/deforestation-regulation-implementation/eudr-cooperation-and-partnerships_en)

### Inferences
- Design for full due diligence on Colombian supply. Any later reclassification to low risk would enable Art. 13 and MSPO status for directly exporting farmers, so the rules engine should key off a configurable country-risk table.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence that an amended benchmarking regulation was adopted in 2026. Re-check EUR-Lex before launch: **verify**.

## Q5. Reference numbers, verification numbers and passing references downstream

### Takeaway
When a DDS is accepted, the system issues a reference number and a verification number. An SD gets a declaration identifier. The operator must give its reference number to the first downstream operator or trader (Art. 4(7)) and to customs on the customs declaration (Art. 26(4)). Under the amended text, later actors do not pass numbers further down, and no one references an earlier DDS inside a new DDS any more.

### Cited Findings
- The DDS reference number goes to direct buyers. The SD identifier goes to first buyers. The first downstream actor keeps them for 5 years and need not pass them on. Re-importers with no supplier reference can use the conventional number 22EU2222222222. — [Green Forum](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/deforestation-regulation-implementation/roles-and-responsibilities_en)
- The reference number must be on the customs declaration before import or export, so the DDS must be filed before the declaration is lodged. One DDS may appear on many customs declarations, and one declaration may list many DDS. — [FAQ v5, Q5.20, Q7.21](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- Downstream exporters use a TARIC certificate code instead of a reference number (Art. 26(4)). — [FAQ v5, Q5.6.1](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- There is no obligation to put reference numbers on invoices or delivery notes. — [FAQ v5, Q7.21](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- The verification number is an extra security layer and need not be shared. Holders of a reference number plus its verification number can see geolocation only if the submitter allows it. Validity can be checked in the system, including by CSV batch. — [FAQ v5, Q3.6, Q3.6.1, Q7.7, Q7.25](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- A DDS can be withdrawn or amended within 72 hours, unless it has already been used in customs, the product has been placed on the market, or a check has been notified (Art. 5 of Implementing Reg. 2024/3084). — [FAQ v5, Q7.6](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- Voluntary grouping of DDS reference numbers is being added to the Information System. — [COM(2026) 191](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a3c5c3a0-232e-43c4-b0b8-1eecb1df45c7_en?filename=Report+from+the+Commission+to+the+Council+and+Parliament+on+the+EUDR.pdf)
- There is no legal obligation to share geolocation along the chain, and data may be shared outside the system. Users can export a DDS to PDF and extract its geolocation. — [FAQ v5, Q7.14, Q7.15](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- EUDR-Snap cannot create reference numbers. Only the EU Information System (TRACES) issues them. The app should let the importer store the returned reference and verification numbers against each lot, so the cooperative's records can link lot, plots and DDS reference.
- Suspected supersession: industry templates built on the 2023 text that "reference upstream DDS" (Annex II point 4, Art. 4(8)-(9)) are **outdated**.

### Gaps
- Final Information System API and webservice specifications for SD and grouping after the June 2026 reopening: **verify** on the Commission IS page (https://green-business.ec.europa.eu/deforestation-regulation-implementation/information-system-deforestation-regulation_en).

## Q6. Commission guidance and FAQ: negligible risk, legality evidence, mixing, plot-level traceability, cooperatives

### Takeaway
The latest texts are FAQ Version 5 (April 2026) and the third-edition Guidance (Commission Notice C/2026/3896, July 2026). Their positions: "negligible risk" is a conclusion reached through due diligence, never a status of a country or commodity. Mass balance is prohibited, but segregation without full identity preservation is allowed. Every plot must be geolocated, with only limited "declaration in excess" for traced bulk. Legality evidence should be proportionate to risk. Cooperatives may act as data intermediaries, but the operator stays responsible.

### Cited Findings
- **Negligible risk:** this is the Art. 2(26) definition, and it is not an exemption. "There is no 'risk status' for each commodity". Coffee or cocoa from a given country cannot be pre-deemed negligible risk. — [FAQ v5, Q5.9, Q5.10, Q5.11](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- **Mass balance:** chains that mix with commodities of unknown origin or non-compliant commodities "are not allowed". Segregation is required at every step, but "full identity preservation is not needed". If one plot in a mixed bulk lot was deforested after 2020, the whole batch is non-compliant. — [FAQ v5, Q1.4, Q1.5](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- **Mixed silos:** declare every plot that fed the silo since it was last emptied, or (for FIFO systems) all inputs up to at least 200% of silo capacity. Declaring only the amount shipped is not allowed. — [FAQ v5, Q1.17](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- **Declaration in excess:** allowed only for fully traced bulk that is not mixed with unknown origin, or for crop rotation. The operator becomes responsible for all declared plots, and one bad plot taints all of them. Regional or country-wide declarations are not in line with the rules. — [FAQ v5, Q1.18](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- **Missing upstream data:** if information cannot be obtained, the operator "must refrain" from placing the product. — [FAQ v5, Q1.27](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- **Legality, proportionate approach (new in 2026):** operators can make a first examination of available information (public reports, indicators, country and region classifications, and the Commission repository). If that shows negligible risk, "operators should not be required to carry out an in-depth data collection". In-depth evidence is prioritised for higher-risk supply chains. — [COM(2026) 191, s. 4.1.3](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a3c5c3a0-232e-43c4-b0b8-1eecb1df45c7_en?filename=Report+from+the+Commission+to+the+Council+and+Parliament+on+the+EUDR.pdf)
- **Certification:** can support risk assessment to the extent it covers the information needed, but does not replace due diligence or shift responsibility. — [FAQ v5, Q5.7](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- **Cooperatives as intermediaries:** operators collect legality and geolocation data "via intermediaries like cooperatives, certification bodies, national traceability systems", and the operators remain "legally responsible". No personal data from farmers is required unless they are direct suppliers. — [FAQ v5, Q1.10](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- **Deforestation-free check:** the reference point is 31 December 2020, looking back at whether the land was forest. No specific satellite tool or resolution is mandated. The EU Observatory 2020 forest map can be used (FAQ s. 9.10). — [FAQ v5, Q5.12, Q5.18](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- **Composite products:** for chocolate (1806), due diligence covers only the cocoa components (cocoa commodity column of Annex I). — [FAQ v5, Q1.3](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- **Guidance edition:** the third-edition Guidance is Commission Notice C/2026/3896, published in the OJ around 20 July 2026, according to [ECF](https://www.ecf-coffee.org/european-union-deforestation-regulation-eudr/) and [EUR-Lex link](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2026/3896/oj). It replaces the earlier Guidance C/2025/3588 cited in the FAQ. I could not retrieve its text.

### Inferences
- The app should enforce segregation in the data model: a lot can only contain plots that are all geolocated and screened, with no "unknown origin" bucket.
- Cooperative collection centres that blend deliveries need silo/lot logic (declare all contributing plots since the last emptying).

### Gaps
- Specific evidence examples per Art. 2(40) letter in Guidance C/2026/3896 could not be read (the EUR-Lex HTML and PDF fetches returned empty): **verify**.

## Q7. Penalties (Art. 25) and the scope delegated act effective 30 Dec 2027

### Takeaway
Member States set the penalties. They must include fines of at least 4% of EU-wide annual turnover for legal persons, confiscation, exclusion from procurement for up to 12 months, temporary market bans, and loss of simplified due diligence. Delegated Regulation 2026/2102 adds soluble coffee (HS 2101 11) from 30 Dec 2027. Green and roasted coffee (0901) and cocoa (1801-1806) stay in scope from 30 Dec 2026 or 30 June 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 25(2) penalties: (a) fines proportionate to environmental damage and product value, with a maximum of "at least 4 %" of total annual Union-wide turnover for legal persons, increasing for repeat infringements; (b) confiscation of products; (c) confiscation of revenues; (d) exclusion of up to 12 months from public procurement and public funding; (e) a temporary prohibition on placing, making available or exporting, for serious or repeated infringements; (f) a prohibition on using simplified due diligence. — [EUDR Art. 25](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1115)
- 2025/2650 extends Art. 25 to downstream operators and traders. Art. 24 corrective actions now include rectifying formal non-compliance, withdrawal, recall, donation or disposal. — [Reg. 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng); [FAQ v5, Q10.2](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- Delegated Reg. 2026/2102 was adopted on 13 July 2026, published in the OJ on 17 Sept 2026 and entered into force on 18 Sept 2026 (secondary sources). Additions applying from 30 Dec 2027: soluble coffee and coffee extracts, essences and concentrates (HS 2101 11), frozen cattle tongues, and further palm oil derivatives (fatty alcohols, glycerol, acids, amines, soap). Removals: cattle hides, skins and leather (4101, 4104, 4107), certain rubber articles and belts (4010, 4016), retreaded tyres, and soya seeds for sowing. — [eudrcheck guide](https://eudrcheck.co.uk/guides/eudr-annex-i-changes-2026-2102); [Green Forum](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/deforestation-regulation-implementation/roles-and-responsibilities_en)
- 2025/2650 itself removed printed products (HS 49) from Annex I. — [Stibbe](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/the-amended-eudr-what-has-changed-and-what-has-remained)

### Inferences
- For EUDR-Snap (green coffee and cocoa beans), the 2027 scope changes matter mainly if buyers are soluble-coffee makers, who become operators or downstream operators for HS 2101 11 from 30 Dec 2027.

### Gaps
- Exact HS codes for the palm derivatives, the effective date of the removals (sources cite 18 Sept or 7 Oct 2026), and whether any cocoa code changed: **verify** against the OJ text of 2026/2102. Each Member State's national penalty law (for example Germany or the Netherlands) was not researched.

## Q8. Templates and standard formats to emulate

### Takeaway
The only "official" formats are the Information System's DDS and SD data structures (the Annex II and Annex III fields), the GeoJSON specification and the TRACES API/webservice. The Commission explicitly offers no risk assessment template. Planned producer-country legislation and certification repositories, with Commission templates for contributors, were due by end-2026.

### Cited Findings
- No pre-set format or question list for due diligence exists. — [FAQ v5, Q5.16](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- The SD follows "existing Due Diligence Statement format", with API support. Repositories will be hosted on dedicated websites, and the Commission "will provide templates" for third countries and scheme owners to contribute. — [COM(2026) 191, s. 4.2, 4.4](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a3c5c3a0-232e-43c4-b0b8-1eecb1df45c7_en?filename=Report+from+the+Commission+to+the+Council+and+Parliament+on+the+EUDR.pdf)
- A GeoJSON file description and technical workarounds are published on the Commission IS page. — [FAQ v5, fn 15](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf); https://green-business.ec.europa.eu/deforestation-regulation-implementation/information-system-deforestation-regulation_en
- DDS text may be in any EU language, but use of the language of the Member State handling it is recommended. — [FAQ v5, Q7.19](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/system/files/media/document/2026-08/FAQ-UPDATE-5th-Iteration-EN.pdf)
- The ITC is developing the two repositories with the Commission. — [Green Forum implementation page](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/deforestation-regulation-implementation_en)

### Inferences (proposed EUDR-Snap output set for a Colombian cooperative selling to an EU importer)
1. **DDS pre-fill package** (for the importer to submit in TRACES): Annex II fields 1-3 plus GeoJSON, with the fixed point 5 text and point 6 signature block left for the operator. Label it clearly as a "draft for the operator", because the cooperative is not the signatory.
2. **Art. 9 information dossier per lot:** product description, quantity, country, plot geolocations, harvest date or season, supplier and customer contacts, deforestation-free evidence, and legality evidence mapped to Art. 2(40)(a)-(h).
3. **Art. 10 risk assessment worksheet:** criteria (a)-(n), each with evidence, finding and rating, plus an overall "negligible / not negligible" conclusion and a review date.
4. **Art. 11 mitigation log**, including smallholder support measures.
5. **Segregation and chain-of-custody record** (lot to plots, no unknown-origin inputs; silo rule).
6. **Retention index** (5 years) and a field to store the DDS reference and verification numbers returned by TRACES.
7. (Only for low-risk-country MSPOs, a future feature) an **Annex III SD draft**.

### Gaps
- No Commission model risk assessment exists, and I did not review industry templates (for example ECF, Rainforest Alliance or Fairtrade toolkits). Fairtrade has a coffee/cocoa scope guide: [Fairtrade EUDR product guide](https://www.fairtrade.net/content/dam/fairtrade/global/fairthrive/EUDR-product%20guide%20on%20coffee%20and%20cocoa%20scope.pdf) (not reviewed).
