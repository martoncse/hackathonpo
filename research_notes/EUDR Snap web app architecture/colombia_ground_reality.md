Scope note: about 20 tool calls. Several primary PDFs (CGIAR July 2026, OECD Feb 2026, GIZ/INCAS Aug 2026) were downloaded and read as text. Some FNC pages could not be fetched (connection reset), so FNC platform status comes from secondary press. Items marked **verify** are uncertain or come from my background knowledge, not a fetched source.

## 1. FNC, SICA and coffee-sector EUDR infrastructure; Fedecacao and cocoa

### Takeaway
For coffee, the FNC's SICA already holds georeferenced data on about 1.8M coffee lots and about 500k families, and the FNC shares coordinates with registered exporters once farmers give written habeas-data consent. Exporters such as Racafé call geolocation "largely solved." That means the gap for coffee is legality evidence, lot-to-plot linkage through cooperatives, and the DDS file, not geolocation. Cocoa has nothing like SICA. Traceability there is private (CNCh, Luker), there is a GIZ/Fedecacao open-source app (MiCacao), and a national cocoa traceability platform is still only a recommendation.

### Cited Findings
**Coffee: FNC / SICA**
- SICA is the FNC's georeferenced database: records on about **1.8 million coffee lots** and socioeconomic data on nearly **500,000 families**. It was created in the early 1990s and the FNC says it is being upgraded with AI — [Mongabay, Mar 2026](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/03/colombias-coffee-industry-well-placed-but-wary-as-eu-deforestation-rule-looms/); [Daily Coffee News, 12 May 2026](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/05/12/as-colombia-preps-for-eudr-challenges-for-smallholders-and-indigenous-growers-remain/)
- More than **1,000 FNC agronomists/technicians** are collecting **written consent** from growers. With that consent, **registered exporters** can access farm coordinates and SICA records to show deforestation-free status to EU buyers — [Daily Coffee News, May 2026](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/05/12/as-colombia-preps-for-eudr-challenges-for-smallholders-and-indigenous-growers-remain/)
- The FNC position is that **exporters, not growers**, carry the burden of showing compliance. Racafé uses SICA to get geospatial data on all its producers. Racafé's Camila Cortes Severino says most producers already have mapped plot polygons and that geolocation is "largely solved" — [Daily Coffee News](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/05/12/as-colombia-preps-for-eudr-challenges-for-smallholders-and-indigenous-growers-remain/)
- Origin of the exporter platform: in Aug 2024 the FNC announced a platform giving exporters access to lot coordinates. It was being tested with some exporters. The FNC had updated more than 50,000 habeas-data permits in SICA and expected **93% of growers to have authorised georeferencing by Dec 2024** — [Agronegocios, 22 Aug 2024](https://www.agronegocios.co/agricultura/fnc-lanzara-plataforma-que-dara-acceso-a-coordenadas-de-lotes-cafeteros-3935059); [FNC news page (not fetchable)](https://federaciondecafeteros.org/listado-noticias/fnc-lanzara-plataforma-que-dara-acceso-a-coordenadas-de-lotes-cafeteros-principal-requisito-para-continuar-exportando-a-europa/). **Verify** the platform's current name, the actual consent rate reached, and whether cooperatives (not only exporters) can log in.
- The FNC is also preparing a legal framework for the sector that reflects the family-based coffee economy (97% of coffee families) and runs due-diligence training on legality — [Agronegocios, Aug 2024](https://www.agronegocios.co/agricultura/fnc-lanzara-plataforma-que-dara-acceso-a-coordenadas-de-lotes-cafeteros-3935059)
- SICA **does not produce a DDS and does not prove legality** by itself (vendor analysis) — [TraceX](https://tracextech.com/eudr-coffee-compliance-colombia/)
- Cenicafé (the FNC research arm) has published an EUDR due-diligence support tool for the legality of Colombian coffee — [Cenicafé publication](https://publicaciones.cenicafe.org/index.php/libros_manuales/article/download/5036/5684/4391) (seen in search results, not read; **verify** contents)
- Cédula Cafetera Inteligente (FNC grower ID, also a bank card): its chip stores **17 variables from SICA** about the farm and family. Cooperatives can pay growers through it ("canal cafetero", since 2015). About 75% of growers hold one, per the FNC's ABC document and Agronegocios — [FNC ABC Cédula Cafetera, Oct 2025](https://federaciondecafeteros.org/app/uploads/2025/10/ABC-CC-V14-Oct.pdf); [Agronegocios](https://www.agronegocios.co/agricultura/todo-lo-que-necesita-saber-sobre-la-cedula-cafetera-y-que-beneficios-tiene-con-ella-3829335) (from search snippets; **verify**)
- Being registered "Activo" in SICA is a precondition for FNC benefits such as the zero-rate credit programme — [FNC T&C Crédito Tasa Cero, May 2026](https://federaciondecafeteros.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Términos-y-Condiciones-Programa-de-Crédito-Tasa-Cero-Vr.-Link-FNC.pdf) (snippet)

**Cocoa: Fedecacao and others**
- The **Mesa Nacional de Trazabilidad del Cacao (MNTC)** was created in 2023 under Fedecacao's leadership as a multi-stakeholder platform — [OECD, Feb 2026](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/02/environmental-sustainability-initiatives-in-the-colombian-cocoa-sector_e8d9aef8/1b5cf384-en.pdf) (search snippet)
- Compañía Nacional de Chocolates (CNCh) and Luker Chocolate run **their own traceability systems**, which "have not been scaled across the broader supply base" — [CGIAR brief, July 2026](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/fa52c0c1-a3bc-4b12-840f-1a47d015ff54/content)
- CNCh launched the **Programa de Trazabilidad y Cero Deforestación (PTCD)** in 2024, covering digital monitoring and certification. The CNCh–Solidaridad "Asómbrate" programme engages about **22,000 cocoa families** in CNCh's supply chain. Eligibility includes 0.5–10 ha and "legal ownership or responsible tenure" — [OECD, Feb 2026](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/02/environmental-sustainability-initiatives-in-the-colombian-cocoa-sector_e8d9aef8/1b5cf384-en.pdf)
- **MiCacao**: an open-source app built by GIZ, Helvetas Peru, FEDECACAO and the Open Food Chain Foundation, used by growers to record cocoa from deforestation-free plots in Huila, Tolima and Santander, among other areas — [search summary citing GIZ/OECD sources](https://www.giz.de/sites/default/files/media/els-document/2026-06/ruta-conformidad-productores-cacao-eudr-compressed_0.pdf) (**verify** the exact source document)
- A GIZ producer guide (June 2026) says the EUDR allows a single GPS point for plots under 4 ha, but recommends polygons as safer because they reflect the real cultivated area — [GIZ, Ruta de conformidad cacao EUDR, 2026-06](https://www.giz.de/sites/default/files/media/els-document/2026-06/ruta-conformidad-productores-cacao-eudr-compressed_0.pdf)
- **Acuerdo Cero Deforestación del cacao**: the "Iniciativa Cacao, Bosques y Paz – Acuerdo Marco para la Acción Conjunta", dated 17 July 2018. Signatories were the Government (MinAmbiente, MinAgricultura), Fedecacao, Casa Luker, IDH, WRI and others, with World Cocoa Foundation support. It came with a 10-year action plan (Plan de Acción 2030) — [IDH Acuerdo Marco PDF](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/uploaded/2018/07/Acuerdo-Marco-Cacao-Bosques-Colombia-FINAL.pdf); [IDH Plan de Acción 2030](https://www.idhsustainabletrade.com/uploaded/2019/12/Plan-de-Accion-2030.pdf); [MinAmbiente](https://www.minambiente.gov.co/colombia-iniciativa-de-cero-deforestacion-del-cacao/); [Radio Nacional](https://radionacional.co/actualidad/medio-ambiente/cacao-libre-de-deforestacion-en-colombia)
- Coffee has a parallel agreement, the **Acuerdo Café, Bosque y Clima** — [MinAmbiente](https://www.minambiente.gov.co/con-el-acuerdo-de-cafe-bosque-y-clima-gobierno-cumple-una-meta-mas-de-su-plan-nacional-de-desarrollo/)
- The EU REDD Facility (EFI) ran a national dialogue on whether Colombia and Ecuador could build a national information system to monitor deforestation risk in cocoa chains — [EU REDD Facility](https://euredd.efi.int/construccion-dialogo-nacional-desarrollo-sistema-trazabilidad-transparencia-cacao-libre-deforestacion/)
- CGIAR (July 2026) recommends a **national cocoa traceability policy and a publicly backed interoperable platform** led by the MNTC and government, plus a **formal farm registration system** integrating ICA, UPRA and MinAgricultura data, anchored in Fedecacao's producer knowledge base. This confirms that no national cocoa system exists yet — [CGIAR brief](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/fa52c0c1-a3bc-4b12-840f-1a47d015ff54/content)

### Inferences
- For coffee, EUDR-Snap should **not re-collect geolocation** for growers already in SICA. A good design imports or references SICA-derived coordinates through the exporter (or the cooperative, if FNC access allows it), with the photo/OCR flow aimed at receipts and legality documents and at linking each purchase lot to its plots.
- Cocoa is the clearer gap: fragmented, no national registry, and only private systems plus MiCacao. A free cooperative tool has more room here, but it should export in a form MiCacao or the MNTC can use instead of creating yet another silo.
- Many FNC-affiliated coffee cooperatives sit inside the FNC institutional system. Their data access probably runs through FNC agreements, so positioning relative to the FNC is a political as well as a technical question (**verify** with a cooperative).

### Gaps
- Current (Nov 2026) name and status of the FNC exporter coordinates platform, the consent rate actually reached, and whether cooperatives or non-registered buyers can access it. The FNC site was not reachable.
- Whether SICA stores polygons or points for all lots. Racafé says "most" have polygons; no official figure was found.
- No official Fedecacao EUDR position statement or Fedecacao registry size was found.

## 2. Colombian government response and EU / donor cooperation

### Takeaway
Government action is fragmented and mostly reactive. UPRA (agricultural frontier) and IDEAM (forest cover) supply reference layers, and the CGIAR diagnostic says public institutions have been "absent from coordination." Most of the momentum comes from EFI and the EU Delegation (the "Exportar sin Deforestar" dialogues, legality guides, pilots), GIZ (INCAS Global+, SAFE, MiCacao) and private exporters. No national EUDR traceability or certification system for coffee or cocoa exists as of Nov 2026. A new cattle traceability law (May 2026) is the only national-level traceability legislation found.

### Cited Findings
- 3rd "**Exportar sin Deforestar**" meeting, 30 Oct 2026, Bogotá: more than 250 stakeholders, organised by EFI with the **EU Delegation in Colombia** (adviser Erik Dhaenens). Systems discussed were IDEAM forest cover, UPRA frontera agrícola, SICA (coffee), the palm geovisor, cocoa traceability records and Satelligence. The main challenge named was **integrating these systems so EU buyers can consult them** — [IFM Noticias, 10 Nov 2026](https://ifmnoticias.com/economicas/colombia-avanza-en-la-consolidacion-de-sistemas-para-garantizar-exportaciones-libres-de-deforestacion-hacia-europa/)
- Preliminary pilot results: palm 99% of evaluated area deforestation-free 2011–2026; **coffee (Huila pilot) 98% of plots meet the EUDR criterion**; cocoa similar across 3 exporters. These figures cover specific areas only — [IFM Noticias](https://ifmnoticias.com/economicas/colombia-avanza-en-la-consolidacion-de-sistemas-para-garantizar-exportaciones-libres-de-deforestacion-hacia-europa/)
- EFI built **legality guides** that map information sources and evidence needed for local-law compliance, tested with 14 organisations in cocoa, coffee and palm. It found that certifications (RSPO, 4C, Rainforest Alliance, Fairtrade, organic) support the DDS but do not replace it. FNC, Fedepalma and Fedecacao called for a government-led international communication strategy — [IFM Noticias](https://ifmnoticias.com/economicas/colombia-avanza-en-la-consolidacion-de-sistemas-para-garantizar-exportaciones-libres-de-deforestacion-hacia-europa/)
- An EFI 2025 document, "Recomendaciones para la debida diligencia en legalidad para Colombia en el marco del Reglamento de la UE sobre productos libres de deforestación", is cited by the GIZ/INCAS study — [INCAS Global+ cost study, Aug 2026](https://www.sustainable-supply-chains.org/fileadmin/INA/Project_Page/INCAS_Global__/Presentacion_Vertical_Cafe.pdf)
- CGIAR finds progress "privately led". Government institutions were "absent from coordination, unclear in their roles, and lacking the mandates or resources" — [CGIAR, July 2026](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/fa52c0c1-a3bc-4b12-840f-1a47d015ff54/content)
- UPRA has published on Colombia's preparation for the EUDR (date not checked) — [UPRA](https://upra.gov.co/es-co/sala-de-prensa/noticias/colombia-se-prepara-para-cumplir-con-el-reglamento-eudr-11152023)
- A **Ley de Ganadería Sostenible y Libre de Deforestación** was reportedly approved on 5 May 2026. It adds traceability and due-diligence duties for auctions, slaughterhouses and exporters (single source; **verify** the law number) — [Sociedad Sostenible](https://sociedadsostenible.co/la-nueva-ley-de-trazabilidad-en-colombia-un-paso-clave-hacia-cadenas-productivas-libres-de-deforestacion/)
- **Team Europe Initiative on Deforestation-free Value Chains**: launched at COP28 on 9 Dec 2023 with €70M, about €86M by Oct 2025 (GIZ slides). A Huila coffee project (Apr 2024–Apr 2026, ended) delivered geolocation and traceability for **200 farms**, a voluntary EUDR compliance agreement, and 160+ producers matched with EU buyers. In Antioquia it produced an EUDR Compliance Agreement and 3 guidebooks — [ICO TW3 EUDR presentation, 27 May 2026](https://www.ico.org/documents/cy2025-26/TW3_EUDR_20260527.pdf); [TEI factsheet](https://international-partnerships.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-12/factsheet-tei-deforestation-free-value-chains-05122023_en.pdf)
- Related EU/donor instruments: EFI Technical Facility on deforestation-free value chains; EUDR Community of Practice (EFI); GIZ "Enabling deforestation-free supply chains" (SAFE); AL-INVEST Verde component 2, covering Andean countries including Colombia — [EFI facility](https://efi.int/partnerships/valuechains); [EFI CoP](https://efi.int/partnerships/EUDRCoP); [GIZ](https://www.giz.de/en/projects/enabling-deforestation-free-supply-chains); [AL-INVEST Verde](https://capacity4dev.europa.eu/projects/al-invest-verde-0/content/104844_en)
- **GIZ INCAS Global+** (BMZ) works on coffee, cocoa and palm in Colombia and commissioned the Amplo Kaya EUDR cost study (Phase I May–Sep 2025, Phase II Apr–Jul 2026) — [INCAS cost study](https://www.sustainable-supply-chains.org/fileadmin/INA/Project_Page/INCAS_Global__/Presentacion_Vertical_Cafe.pdf)
- GIZ guide aimed at small coffee producer organisations in Colombia (June 2026) — [GIZ guía SDD EUDR](https://www.giz.de/sites/default/files/media/els-document/2026-06/gui-uea-sdd-eudr-para-productores-libres-de-deforestacio-uen_0.pdf) (not read in full)
- Other cocoa actors: GEF-backed Landscape Investment Development Hub (reVive); Cacao, Bosques y Paz platform — [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/02/environmental-sustainability-initiatives-in-the-colombian-cocoa-sector_e8d9aef8/1b5cf384-en.pdf); [CGIAR](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/fa52c0c1-a3bc-4b12-840f-1a47d015ff54/content)

### Inferences
- The integration problem EFI names ("so EU buyers can consult them") is the space a free tool can fill, as long as it consumes IDEAM/UPRA layers and SICA data instead of replacing them. INCAS recommends prioritising IDEAM/UPRA national layers plus GFW as a contractual "layer policy." Whisp results should sit alongside these layers, not compete with them.
- Funders likely to back a free cooperative tool: GIZ (INCAS Global+, SAFE), EFI / EU Delegation, TEI partners, IDH (cocoa). This is an inference; no open call was found.

### Gaps
- No MinAgricultura or MinAmbiente resolution creating a national EUDR traceability or certification system was found.
- No AFD Colombia EUDR programme, "EU Smallholder Support" facility, or "Zero Deforestation Hub" entry specific to Colombia was found (**verify**).
- No Global Gateway Colombia EUDR line item was found.

## 3. Size of the target

### Takeaway
Coffee: about 540k families, about 842k ha, 96% of producers with 5 ha or less, average farm about 1.4–1.7 ha. About a quarter of exports go to Europe. Around 32 FNC-network coffee cooperatives, the largest with 2,000–8,000 members each. Cocoa: much smaller, roughly 38k+ smallholders (older figure) on plots averaging about 3 ha, about 194k ha.

### Cited Findings
- Coffee 2024: about **842,000 ha** and **more than 540,000 coffee families**, **96% small producers**. GIZ/INCAS characterises the typical producer as **≈1.4 ha**, with 96% at 5 ha or less — [INCAS Global+ cost study, Aug 2026](https://www.sustainable-supply-chains.org/fileadmin/INA/Project_Page/INCAS_Global__/Presentacion_Vertical_Cafe.pdf)
- More than 800,000 ha in 23 of 32 departments; about 70% of coffee produced by smallholders; more than 76% family-based economies; about 2.5M jobs; **about a quarter of coffee exports go to Europe** — [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/03/colombias-coffee-industry-well-placed-but-wary-as-eu-deforestation-rule-looms/)
- More than 500,000 coffee farms, average 1.7 ha; the largest cooperatives handle **2,000–8,000 members** (vendor blog) — [CleantechHUB](https://cleantechhub.net/eudr-que-es-como-afecta-cafe-colombiano-2026/) (**verify**)
- SICA historic figure: 541,518 producers with 665,950 farms (cut-off 30 Sep 2017) — [Cenicafé (search snippet)](https://publicaciones.cenicafe.org/index.php/atl/article/download/2962/2951)
- **32 coffee cooperatives** named as beneficiaries of an FNC–Davivienda agreement (La República, 2025) — [La República](https://www.larepublica.co/finanzas/davivienda-y-la-federacion-nacional-de-cafeteros-anuncian-la-nueva-cedula-cafetera-4015110) (snippet; **verify**. These are FNC-network "Cooperativas de Caficultores". Many more producer associations exist.)
- FNC operates in more than 600 of 1,103 municipalities — [Daily Coffee News](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/05/12/as-colombia-preps-for-eudr-challenges-for-smallholders-and-indigenous-growers-remain/)
- Cocoa: about 95% of production from small and medium farms averaging **3 ha**, with **38,000+ smallholders** (Abbott et al., 2018). About **194,428 ha** in 2021 (USDA). 10th-largest producer globally. Target of 126,000 t by 2030 — [OECD, Feb 2026](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/02/environmental-sustainability-initiatives-in-the-colombian-cocoa-sector_e8d9aef8/1b5cf384-en.pdf)
- Cocoa: farms of 3 ha or less, 80–90% of producers small, "brechas significativas en georreferenciación y gobernanza de datos" — [INCAS study](https://www.sustainable-supply-chains.org/fileadmin/INA/Project_Page/INCAS_Global__/Presentacion_Vertical_Cafe.pdf)
- Cocoa departments with recent forest-cover change (Caquetá, Putumayo, Guaviare, Meta) face direct exclusion risk — [CGIAR](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/fa52c0c1-a3bc-4b12-840f-1a47d015ff54/content)
- Land titling: "many farms lack formal land titles"; tenure is described as a major legal challenge — [TraceX](https://tracextech.com/eudr-coffee-compliance-colombia/); [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/03/colombias-coffee-industry-well-placed-but-wary-as-eu-deforestation-rule-looms/). UPRA publishes an **Índice de Informalidad** of rural property (2019, 2020 editions) — [UPRA índice](https://upra.gov.co/sites/default/files/publicaciones/documentos//indice_de_informalidad.pdf). The widely quoted figure of roughly **52.7% of rural properties presumed informal** (UPRA 2019) is **verify**: the PDF could not be fetched.
- Rural *labour* informality is 83.4% (Apr–Jun 2025, UPRA). This is not land tenure — [UPRA employment bulletin](https://upra.gov.co/sites/default/files/2025-08/Empleo%20julio%20de%202025.pdf)

### Inferences
- The cooperative customer base is small in count (dozens of FNC cooperatives plus hundreds of associations) but each manages thousands of farms, so the tool has to handle batch workflows, not single-farm entry.
- With about 25% of coffee going to the EU, cooperatives sell to both EU and non-EU buyers. Segregation ("apto EUDR" vs. not) has to be a first-class data concept.

### Gaps
- Current cocoa producer count: the 38k figure dates from 2018. Fedecacao figures of 50–65k families are commonly cited (**verify**).
- No sourced share of coffee or cocoa farms with formal title was found.
- Total number of coffee producer associations (beyond FNC cooperatives) was not found.

## 4. Practical pain points 2025–2026

### Takeaway
The pain is legality (land tenure, informal labour), keeping the lot-to-plot link through aggregation, inconsistent EU buyer demands (different layers and formats), data governance (associations losing access to polygons funded by donors; farmers fearing misuse), costs falling on associations and exporters, and field capture in dispersed low-connectivity areas. Indigenous and unassociated smallholders risk being "invisible" to compliance infrastructure.

### Cited Findings
- GIZ/INCAS cost study (Aug 2026, coffee), median **cost per producer per year**: direct ≈ **$388,121** (range 349k–427k), indirect ≈ **$200,093**, total ≈ **$588,215** (529k–647k). Currency is presumably COP, roughly EUR 120–140 (**verify** currency). Split is 66% direct and 34% indirect, 58% OPEX and 42% CAPEX. The largest items are field technicians for coordinates and deforestation analysis, warehouse segregation, traceability software (about 140,800/producer, non-recurring), drones and hardware. A cost-by-actor chart shows a heavy concentration (93%) on one link, apparently exporters (**verify**) — [INCAS Global+ / Amplo Kaya](https://www.sustainable-supply-chains.org/fileadmin/INA/Project_Page/INCAS_Global__/Presentacion_Vertical_Cafe.pdf)
- INCAS pain points and recommendations:
  - Field polygon capture is "el mayor cuello de botella en zonas dispersas y con baja conectividad". The study recommends a single mapping standard (offline app, minimum precision, metadata, QA), capture in waves by volume and risk, and temporary brigades.
  - "Asociaciones pierden acceso a polígonos financiados por cooperación; productores temen un mal uso de la información". The study recommends shared ownership of shapefiles/GeoJSON and an informed-consent protocol.
  - Buyers re-validate with different layers. The study recommends contractually agreed layer policies, guides on consuming JSON, and an ETL with lot–polygon matching and anti-duplication.
  - "Software integral" is costly and creates lock-in. The study recommends a low-cost stack and interoperability tests with anchor clients.
  - Verifying titles for the whole base is unworkable. The study recommends a standardised legal questionnaire with risk-based document sampling.
  - Many associations don't export directly.
  - Costs fall "de manera asimétrica en asociaciones y pequeños productores".
  - The study also recommends a traffic-light risk triage, with second visits or drones only on alerts.
  - Source: [INCAS study](https://www.sustainable-supply-chains.org/fileadmin/INA/Project_Page/INCAS_Global__/Presentacion_Vertical_Cafe.pdf)
- Lots pass through collection centres, cooperatives and private buyers, and each step is a mixing risk. Seasonal harvest labour is often informal — [TraceX](https://tracextech.com/eudr-coffee-compliance-colombia/); [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/03/colombias-coffee-industry-well-placed-but-wary-as-eu-deforestation-rule-looms/)
- Intermediaries take about 60% of profits and sometimes refuse to share information (AGROSEC). Indigenous communities lack the channels needed for traceability (Kogui and Casa de Paz Shinawindua). There is uncertainty about EU platforms, mapping standards and legality definitions. Rainforest Alliance says data collection raises farmer costs, while Sucafina/STA's Tamari says buyers pay — [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/03/colombias-coffee-industry-well-placed-but-wary-as-eu-deforestation-rule-looms/); [Daily Coffee News](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/05/12/as-colombia-preps-for-eudr-challenges-for-smallholders-and-indigenous-growers-remain/)
- Cocoa: smallholders without buyer relationships or associations are "effectively invisible to compliance infrastructure". Producers in collective territories and legally designated environmental areas, in conflict zones, and in cadmium-affected areas face structural barriers — [CGIAR](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/fa52c0c1-a3bc-4b12-840f-1a47d015ff54/content)
- Vendor price point: EUR 75–150 per DDS (Origo, vendor claim) — [CleantechHUB](https://cleantechhub.net/eudr-funding-smallholder-farmers-dfis-2026/) (**verify**)

### Inferences
- EUDR-Snap should work offline-first with deferred sync. It should export standard GeoJSON plus a DDS-ready JSON that buyers can test. It should store consent records per farmer and make cooperative data ownership explicit (export-anytime) to answer the "lost polygons" complaint directly.
- Legality support should follow the proportional model: a standard questionnaire with photos of documents only as risk-based samples, not mandatory OCR of every title.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on smartphone ownership, literacy or connectivity among Colombian coffee or cocoa growers was found (**verify**, e.g. DANE, CRC or FNC surveys).
- No documented data-ownership disputes between FNC and cooperatives were found.

## 5. Data protection (Ley 1581/2012, Decreto 1377/2013) and GDPR

### Takeaway
Farmer names, IDs, plot coordinates and photos of land documents linked to an identifiable person are personal data under Ley 1581. Processing needs prior, express, informed authorisation, which the FNC is already collecting at scale as "habeas data" permits. Transfers to the EU are governed by Art. 26 plus SIC rules, including the model contracts in SIC Circular Externa 003 of 2025. The EU-side importer is subject to GDPR.

### Cited Findings
- Ley 1581 **Art. 26** prohibits international transfers to countries without adequate protection unless an exception applies or the SIC issues a declaration of conformity — [CMS expert guide](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-data-protection-and-cyber-security-laws/colombia); [Ley 1581](https://www.unidadvictimas.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/Documentos/ley/Ley_1581_2012.pdf)
- **SIC Circular Externa 003 de 2025** adopts model contractual clauses for international transfer and transmission of personal data, especially to destinations without adequacy recognition. One analysis notes the clauses do not remove the prior Art. 26 test — [SIC CE 003/2025](https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/sites/default/files/normativa/Circular%20Externa%20003%20de%202025.pdf); [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/resources/circular-698654caf0ce8430094773.pdf)
- Decreto 1074 de 2015 (which incorporates Decreto 1377/2013) distinguishes **transfer** (to another controller) from **transmission** (to a processor). Decreto 255 de 2022 regulates binding corporate rules — [Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/co/en/who-we-are/news-publications-and-media/our-news-and-publications/tax-and-legal-news/superintendence-of-industry-and-commerce); [Decreto 1377](https://normograma.invima.gov.co/normograma/docs/pdf/decreto_1377_2013.pdf)
- A bill to reform Ley 1581 (Cámara 214 de 2025) exists; its status was not checked — [Congreso Visible](https://apicongresovisible.uniandes.edu.co/uploads/proyecto-ley/14922/1501/25.pdf)
- The FNC collects habeas-data consent before sharing coordinates with exporters — [Agronegocios](https://www.agronegocios.co/agricultura/fnc-lanzara-plataforma-que-dara-acceso-a-coordenadas-de-lotes-cafeteros-3935059)

### Inferences (verify with counsel)
- SIC Circular Externa 005 of 2017 is believed to list EU/EEA states as adequate destinations, which would simplify Colombia-to-EU transfers (**verify**).
- If EUDR-Snap hosts the data, it is likely a *encargado* (processor) for the cooperative, the *responsable*. Needed: a published Política de Tratamiento, a consent capture flow (Spanish, plain language, purpose/retention/rights), RNBD registration if thresholds apply (**verify**), and a DPA with EU-side users.
- On the EU side, an importer receiving farmer-identifiable geolocation is a GDPR controller. EUDR Art. 31 also requires 5-year record retention, which conflicts with data minimisation and should be documented as the legal basis (legal obligation).
- Cédula numbers and ID photos are personal data. Health or ethnic data (Indigenous status) could be sensitive under Art. 5, so it is better not to collect them.

### Gaps
- No source specifically analysing farmer geolocation under Ley 1581 in an EUDR context was found.

## 6. Common paper documents in the coffee/cocoa chain

### Takeaway
The main documents are the purchase receipt or factura issued by the cooperative or buyer, the Cédula Cafetera (FNC ID card with SICA-linked data), and the land document (certificado de tradición y libertad, escritura, or informal proof such as a sana posesión letter or contrato de compraventa). Field lists below are mostly from general knowledge. None of the fetched sources gave an official template.

### Cited Findings
- The Cédula Cafetera chip holds 17 SICA variables on the farm and family, and serves as the payment channel at cooperatives — [FNC ABC](https://federaciondecafeteros.org/app/uploads/2025/10/ABC-CC-V14-Oct.pdf); [Agronegocios](https://www.agronegocios.co/agricultura/todo-lo-que-necesita-saber-sobre-la-cedula-cafetera-y-que-beneficios-tiene-con-ella-3829335) (snippets)
- Documents EUDR due diligence needs per plot or producer: land-tenure documents, environmental permits, labour records, written SICA consent, geolocation (polygons above 4 ha), volume records at collection centres and dry mills, and a 5-year evidence file per shipment — [TraceX](https://tracextech.com/eudr-coffee-compliance-colombia/)
- INCAS recommends against demanding paperwork when there are no risk indicators. It recommends escalating to "certificados, actas" only on overlap or alert — [INCAS study](https://www.sustainable-supply-chains.org/fileadmin/INA/Project_Page/INCAS_Global__/Presentacion_Vertical_Cafe.pdf)

### Inferences (field lists, all **verify** against real samples)
- **Recibo/factura de compra de café**: buyer (cooperative/punto de compra, NIT), date, seller name and cédula, farm name or SICA code (sometimes), vereda/municipio, product (café pergamino seco), kg, factor de rendimiento / quality, price per kg or carga, total, retenciones and contribución cafetera, and whether it is an electronic factura (DIAN) or a paper receipt.
- **Certificado de tradición y libertad** (SNR): folio de matrícula inmobiliaria, círculo registral, property description and boundaries (linderos), area, cédula catastral, chain of owners (anotaciones), liens and encumbrances, date issued.
- **Cédula cafetera**: grower name, cédula number, card number; SICA farm codes are linked through the chip and the SICA lookup.
- Cocoa receipts are similar (kg of cacao seco, grade per NTC 1252).

### Gaps
- No sourced sample templates for cooperative receipts or official field lists were found. A real cooperative should be asked for anonymised samples.

## 7. Existing commercial/NGO tools in Colombia

### Takeaway
The landscape is crowded. SICA (FNC) covers coffee geolocation. Exporters' own systems (Racafé, CNCh, Luker) cover their supply bases. Vendor platforms (Koltiva, TraceX, Origo, TrueData, Rainforest Alliance tools) and the open-source MiCacao (cocoa) and INATrace (used in Honduras, not found in Colombia) all compete. No published pricing was found except vendor claims (EUR 75–150 per DDS, Origo).

### Cited Findings
- **Koltiva (KoltiTrace)**: claims more than 25,274 smallholder coffee producers digitally validated across 8 Latin American countries, including Colombia (vendor claim) — [Koltiva](https://www.koltiva.com/post/weather-tariffs-and-crop-yields-the-triple-risk-facing-brazil-s-coffee-chain)
- **Rainforest Alliance**: offers EUDR support including automated deforestation-risk maps, and calls Colombia one of the best-prepared coffee countries — [Rainforest Alliance EUDR](https://www.rainforest-alliance.org/business/eudr/); [RA Colombia](https://www.rainforest-alliance.org/regions/colombia/)
- **INATrace** (open source): a CGIAR study covers adoption by Honduran coffee farmers. No Colombian deployment was found — [CGIAR Honduras](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/items/c129b79f-0c26-48a5-8a74-d6fd3d03873e)
- **MiCacao** (open source, GIZ/Helvetas/Fedecacao/Open Food Chain) — see section 1
- **Solidaridad**: CNCh–Solidaridad "Asómbrate" programme (about 22k cocoa families) with Rabobank ACORN carbon payments — [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/02/environmental-sustainability-initiatives-in-the-colombian-cocoa-sector_e8d9aef8/1b5cf384-en.pdf)
- Colombian vendors and others: TrueData (Colombian coffee traceability, EUDR evidence, carbon MRV) — [truedata.id](https://truedata.id/); TraceX — [tracextech.com](https://tracextech.com/eudr-coffee-compliance-colombia/); SiembraViva and Biopaisaje (EUDR guidance or services) — [SiembraViva](https://www.siembraviva.com/blog/eudr-cafe-cacao-colombia); [Biopaisaje](https://biopaisaje.eco/blog/eudr-colombia-exportadores-2026); Satelligence (used with IDEAM data for palm) — [IFM Noticias](https://ifmnoticias.com/economicas/colombia-avanza-en-la-consolidacion-de-sistemas-para-garantizar-exportaciones-libres-de-deforestacion-hacia-europa/)
- WUR studies on Colombian coffee and palm under the EUDR — [WUR news](https://www.wur.nl/en/news/stronger-and-more-sustainable-future-colombian-coffee-and-palm-oil-under-eudr)

### Inferences
- The differentiator open to a free tool is not geolocation or satellite checks, which are commoditised and already covered by SICA. It is (a) digitising paper receipts so each purchase is tied to SICA plots for mass balance and segregation, (b) proportional legality evidence capture, and (c) clean, buyer-agnostic export (GeoJSON plus EU TRACES-compatible DDS data) with cooperative-owned data. That matches the INCAS recommendations on interoperability and against lock-in.

### Gaps
- No pricing found for Farmforce, Sourcemap, Koltiva or TechnoServe tools in Colombia, and no Colombian deployments of Farmforce or Sourcemap were confirmed. A targeted vendor search would be needed.
